Below I go through the points raised, grouping by substance and indicating accept/refute. Where three reviewers raise the same point, I treat it once.

1. The “2.800 Jahre Diaspora” claim#

Accept — this is the most serious error and all three reviewers caught it.

The arithmetic is simply wrong. With my own dating (Kanaan/Judäa 1200 BCE–135 CE), the land-based period is ~1.335 years minus ~50 years Babylonian exile, plus 77 years modern Israel ≈ 1.400 years of presence with at least partial autonomy, against 1.813 years (135–1948) of dominant diaspora. “2.800 Jahre Diaspora” would require the founding period to have lasted 120 years.

Fix: Replace the sentence with: “Von den rund 3.200 Jahren seit der israelitischen Landnahme entfiel die längere Hälfte – etwa 1.800 Jahre, von 135 bis 1948 – auf die Diaspora als dominanten Zustand.” This keeps the rhetorical force of the asymmetry without the false precision.

There is a deeper conceptual problem the reviewers expose (Reviewer B, Reviewer C): my definition of “Diaspora-Charakter” conflates fremde Herrschaft with fremder Boden. Judäa under Rome was foreign rule on home soil; Babylon was foreign rule on foreign soil. Accept. I will define diaspora strictly as life outside the land of Israel, which makes the opening count clean and defensible, and note separately that even the homeland spent most of its history under foreign suzerainty.

2. Souveränität seit 135 CE (Sektion 14)#

Accept in part, refute in part.

Reviewer A is right that sovereignty ended earlier — de facto 63 BCE (Pompeius), formally 6 CE (province). 135 CE marks the end of autonomous Jewish political-military existence in the land, not of sovereignty.

Reviewer C adds the Hasmonean kingdom (140–63 BCE) and the Bar-Kochba para-state as counterexamples to “first sovereignty since 135 CE.” This is partly a misreading: I wrote “seit 135 CE,” i.e. the first sovereign Jewish polity after that date, which is correct. The Hasmoneans predate it; Bar-Kochba is the 135 endpoint.

Fix: Change to “das erste Zentrum mit staatlicher Souveränität seit dem Ende des hasmonäischen Reiches” or, more cleanly, “die erste jüdische Staatlichkeit seit der Antike.” This sidesteps the 135-vs-63-BCE quibble entirely.

I refute Reviewer C’s claim that Israel has “keine unumstrittene Souveränität” because of the occupied territories. Contested borders do not negate sovereignty within recognised territory; Israel is a UN member state. This is a category error, conflating sovereignty with the legitimacy of specific territorial control.

3. “Totale Vertreibung” 135 CE (Sektion 1)#

Accept. Reviewer B is correct: there was no total deportation. Substantial Jewish populations remained in Galilee (the Mishnah and Jerusalem Talmud were produced there over the following centuries — this is even implied by my own text in Sektion 5).

Fix: “135 CE die Niederschlagung des Bar-Kochba-Aufstands, der Ausschluss der Juden aus Jerusalem und das Ende jüdischer politischer Existenz in Judäa; das Schwergewicht verlagerte sich nach Galiläa und in die Diaspora.”

4. Syria Palaestina — “Auslöschung des jüdischen Namens”#

Accept the qualification. Reviewers B and C: the punitive intent is the mainstream reading but not certain, and “Auslöschung” overstates — Judäa survived as a name in Jewish usage.

Fix: “Hadrian benennt die Provinz in Syria Palaestina um – in der Forschung meist als Strafmaßnahme gedeutet.” Drop “bewusste Auslöschung.”

5. “Rabbinen” in the Second Temple period#

Accept. Reviewer B: anachronistic. The rabbinic movement crystallised after 70 CE. Fix: “Schriftgelehrte und früh-rabbinische Kreise.”

6. Perserzeit: “Synagoge entstand,” “kanonische Form”#

Accept, with care. Reviewers B and C: “die synagogale Tradition entstand” is too pointed; institutionalisation is safer. And canonisation was a long process (Esra/Nehemia ~450 BCE; Tanakh fixed ~100 CE), not completed under Persia.

Fix: “Unter persischer Herrschaft verstetigte sich eine torazentrierte Frömmigkeit, die ohne Tempel und Land auskommt; die kanonische Fixierung der Hebräischen Bibel zog sich über Jahrhunderte hin und war erst um 100 CE abgeschlossen.”

I partly refute Reviewer C’s “synagogale Tradition entstand parallel zum Tempelkult, nicht als Ersatz.” Both are true and not in tension: it arose alongside the Temple and proved to be the portable substitute once the Temple fell. That is precisely my point. I will phrase it so both aspects stand.

7. Mizrachim vs. Sephardim (Sektionen 2, 5)#

Accept. This is a real terminological sloppiness flagged by Reviewers A and B. Babylonian and Persian Jews are the proto-Mizrachim, not Sephardim. They later adopted Sephardic liturgy but never saw Spanish soil.

Fix: Relabel Sektion 2 and Sektion 5 consistently as Mizrachim, and add one sentence distinguishing the ethnic-historical term (Mizrachim) from the later liturgical spread of the Sephardic rite, which is the source of the common confusion.

8. Babylon: Exilarch, Operation Esra/Nehemia#

Accept the precision, refute the overcorrection.

Reviewer C is right that the Exilarch was a mediator with circumscribed power, not an “Oberhaupt” in the modern sense. Fix: “als offizieller Repräsentant der Juden gegenüber den sassanidischen Behörden anerkannt.”

On “fast alle”: ~120.000–130.000 of ~150.000 left in 1950–51. That is “fast alle” — ~85%, and today ~2.000 remain. Refute the implication that “fast alle” is wrong; I will add the figures so the reader can judge.

9. Alexandria: numbers, Philo, scope of the 115–117 revolt#

Accept the uncertainty flag. The 100.000–200.000 / “one third” figures (Reviewers B, C) are ancient guesses. Fix: “geschätzt — die Zahlen sind unsicher — in der Größenordnung von 100.000.”

Refute the “ohne Nachkommen” objection partly. Reviewer B is right that the formulation is too absolute; Romaniote and Hellenistic strands persisted (though not from Alexandria). Fix: “ein eigener Zweig, der keine direkte Nachfolgegemeinschaft hinterließ, dessen Elemente aber nachwirkten.”

On Philo (Reviewer C): I partly accept. “Synthese” is defensible — Philo genuinely fused allegory with Middle Platonism — but the point that his influence ran to Christianity (Logos/Johannesprolog) more than to Judaism is correct and worth adding. Fix: keep “Synthese,” add the asymmetric reception.

On the revolt’s scope (Reviewer C): correct that it centred on Alexandria and Cyrene (the Kitos War), and that Egyptian Jewish presence ended only with 640 CE. My text already says both. I’ll tighten to avoid suggesting the 115–117 revolt ended the whole community.

10. Römisches Reich: 4–8 million, “Religionsfreiheit,” “Dhimmi”#

Accept all three. This is the clearest case where my own statistical scruple (visible in the Nobel section) was not applied.

  • Numbers: The 4–8 million figure descends from Harnack and is heavily contested; Seth Schwartz and others favour 1–2 million (Reviewers A, B, C). Fix: “historisch stark umstrittene Schätzungen, die von rund 1–2 Millionen bis zu 4–8 Millionen reichen.” I’ll carry the same caveat into the closing demographic note.

  • “Religionsfreiheit” / “religio licita”: anachronistic (Reviewer B, C). Fix: “traditionell geduldeter Kult mit Privilegien — Sabbatruhe, Befreiung vom Kaiserkult und vom Militärdienst.”

  • “Dhimmi” applied to Rome (Sektion 4): I did not use Dhimmi in Sektion 4 — Reviewer C misread; I used it correctly for the Islamic world (Sektion 6). Refute. But I will make the Roman status-language cleaner anyway, per the previous point.

11. Arabische Welt: 850.000 emigrants#

Accept the framing concern. The figure is contested and politically loaded (Reviewer B). Fix: “schätzungsweise 800.000–1.000.000, je nach Zählweise und Zeitraum.”

12. Spanien / al-Andalus: romanticisation, Almohaden, Maimonides#

Accept. Reviewer C is right to puncture the harmony narrative:

  • Add the Granada massacre of 1066 and note that the Almohaden (from 1146) forced conversion or flight long before 1492.

  • Maimonides fled to Egypt (1165), becoming physician at Saladin’s court — I imply he stayed in the Islamic world generally; I’ll name Egypt.

Fix: insert one sentence: “Das Goldene Zeitalter war regional und zeitlich begrenzt (etwa 10.–12. Jh.); Gewalt gab es auch unter muslimischer Herrschaft (Pogrom von Granada 1066), und die Almohaden erzwangen ab 1146 Konversion oder Flucht.”

13. Osmanisches Reich: Thessaloniki “mehrheitlich jüdisch,” grammar, Romanioten#

Accept the grammar and spelling: “das große Auffangbecken,” “Romanioten” (Reviewers A/B).

Partly refute on Thessaloniki. In the 16th century Jews were a plurality and at times a slim majority of the city; “die einzige mehrheitlich jüdische Stadt in Europa seit der Antike” is defensible for that window but not for 1900 (~37%). Fix: “wurde im 16. Jahrhundert zeitweise zur Stadt mit jüdischer Bevölkerungsmehrheit” — bounded in time, which is honest.

14. Westeuropa: “finsteres Mittelalter,” Raschi, SchUM, Emanzipationsdaten#

  • “finsteres Mittelalter”: Accept. Historiographically dated; I used it with irony (quotation marks) to mark the contrast with flourishing Babylon, but I’ll set it off more clearly as a contemporary cliché I’m deliberately invoking, or drop it.

  • Raschi: Refute the “error.” I wrote that Raschi “wirkte in Troyes” — exactly Reviewer C’s correction. The SchUM cities and Troyes are listed as separate items; no claim that Raschi worked in SchUM. No change needed beyond ensuring the sentence can’t be misread.

  • Emanzipationsdaten: Accept that emancipation was gradual and uneven (already implied by my listing 1791/1867/1871); I’ll add “ein ungleichmäßiger, jahrzehntelanger Prozess.”

15. Polen-Litauen: Chmelnizki numbers, Vaad, “Vernichtungslager in Polen”#

Chmelnizki: The reviewers disagree among themselves — A says 18.000–20.000 (Stampfer), B says “Zehntausende (40–100k),” C says 100.000–200.000. This spread is exactly why “Hunderttausende” is irresponsible. Accept the correction toward the modern low estimates. Fix: “nach moderner Forschung in der Größenordnung von einigen Zehntausend (Schätzungen reichen von ~18.000 bis über 100.000) — die zeitgenössischen Chroniken nannten weit höhere Zahlen.” This shows the historiographical revision rather than picking one number.

Vaad Arba Aratzot: Partly refute. “Quasi-Parlament” is a deliberate analogy, not a claim of state power. But Reviewers A’s and C’s caution is fair: its competence was fiscal, juridical (halacha) and educational, not sovereign. Fix: “eine Form jüdischer Selbstverwaltung mit Kompetenzen in Steuer-, Rechts- und Bildungsfragen — kein Parlament im staatlichen Sinn, aber in der Diaspora vorher wie nachher ohne Beispiel.” Keep the analogy, fence it.

“Vernichtungslager lagen in Polen”: Accept the crucial precision (Reviewer B). Fix: “die deutschen Vernichtungslager im besetzten Polen.” The distinction is not pedantic; the formulation matters.

16. Russland: Jiddisch-Verbot, dates, Birobidzhan#

Accept — clear factual error, flagged by A and B. The Soviets promoted Yiddish as proletarian culture in the 1920s–30s (schools, newspapers, courts, GOSET theatre); they banned Hebrew as religious/Zionist; Yiddish institutions were liquidated only in the late-Stalinist campaign (1948–52).

Fix: “ohne Religion (Synagogen weitgehend geschlossen), Hebräisch als Sprache des Zionismus kriminalisiert; die zunächst geförderten jiddischen Kulturinstitutionen wurden im späten Stalinismus ab 1948 zerschlagen.”

Ansiedlungsrayon dates: Accept — formally established 1791, not 1772; the partitions (1772–95) created the population. Fix the date.

Birobidzhan: Accept as a worthwhile addition — a failed experiment in territorial Jewish autonomy fits the chapter’s logic. One or two sentences.

Herzl: Accept. Born and raised in Budapest, active in Vienna. Fix: “Herzl stammte aus Budapest und wirkte in Wien.”

17. USA: first wave, “Assimilationskosten,” demography#

Accept the German-Jewish wave (1820–1880). Reviewer C is right; my text jumps from 1654 to 1880 and skips the German-Jewish immigration that built Reform Judaism and much of American Jewish institutional life. Fix: add it as a distinct phase between founding and the East European mass migration.

“ohne Assimilationskosten (Konversion, Namensänderung)”: Accept. Reviewer B is right — there were name changes, conversions, very high intermarriage. My point was about state-coerced assimilation. Fix: “ohne den staatlich erzwungenen Assimilationsdruck des europäischen Modells; soziale Anpassungskosten blieben.”

Demography: estimates range 6–7.5 million depending on definition. Fix: “geschätzt 6–7,5 Millionen, je nach Definition” — and note that the definitional question (who counts as Jewish) is the same one that haunts the Nobel statistics.

“keine staatliche Verfolgung”: I’ll keep this — it is the structurally decisive point and remains true — but tie it to the definitional honesty above.

18. Lateinamerika: current numbers#

Accept. Argentina is ~170.000–180.000 today, not 400.000 (Reviewer B); Brazil ~120.000. Fix the figures. The 300.000 mid-century Buenos Aires figure stands as historical peak.

19. Israel: numbers, non-halachic immigrants#

Accept the refinements. ~7,1–7,2 million is right. Add that a substantial share of the 1990s Soviet immigrants were not halachically Jewish — which sharpens my own point about internal tensions and the rabbinate. Fix: one clause.

20. Persien: “einziges Land mit jüdischer Gemeinschaft bis heute”#

Accept. Factually wrong — Turkey (~15.000, which I myself state in Sektion 8) and Morocco also retain communities. Fix: “eine der wenigen größeren Gemeinschaften der Region, die bis heute fortbesteht.”

On the Iran “Vernichtungsrhetorik” (Reviewer B): I’ll keep the substance — the eliminationist rhetoric against Israel is documented — but phrase it precisely: “wiederholte Eliminationsdrohungen gegen Israel bei gleichzeitiger Duldung der eigenen jüdischen Gemeinde.” The paradox is real and worth stating without inflation.

21. Nobel excursus#

Baeyer: Accept — outright error. Adolf von Baeyer was not Jewish. Fix: replace with Paul Ehrlich (Medicine 1908) or Richard Willstätter (Chemistry 1915). Embarrassing; thank the reviewer.

The 220 figure / “who counts as Jewish”: Accept the methodological demand (B and C). The count depends entirely on the definition (halachic vs. one Jewish parent). Fix: state the criterion explicitly (“mindestens ein jüdischer Elternteil”), give the range (~180 halachic vs. ~220 inclusive), and cite the basis (e.g. the commonly used compilations — I will name the source rather than present 220 as a hard fact). This also forces honesty about Bohr, whom I list — Jewish mother, so halachically Jewish, but the point stands that the criterion must be declared.

Category percentages: Accept — these need a source and a stated method. Fix: footnote the source and the definition.

“Manhattan-Projekt wesentlich ein Werk der Emigranten”: Accept the overstatement (B). The émigrés were decisive in specific areas, but Oppenheimer’s project rested heavily on US-born scientists and vast industrial effort. Fix: “maßgeblich mitgeprägt von emigrierten europäischen Wissenschaftlern.”

The missing fourth factor — urbanisation / network effects: Accept (A, B, C all converge here, and it is the strongest substantive addition). Post-emancipation Jews were concentrated in exactly the urban bourgeois milieu with the highest educational density and best access to research institutions; and scientific patronage networks (the émigré physicists promoting each other) compounded it. This strengthens my anti-essentialist argument by supplying a further structural mechanism. Fix: add it as a fourth factor, explicitly framed as path-dependence rather than disposition.

Counterexamples (Bene Israel, Cochin, Kaifeng): Accept the logical point (C). My three factors are necessary, not sufficient; communities that were emancipated but lacked the urban-academic concentration produced no such output. This is not a refutation of my thesis — it confirms that concentration in the exception-stations is doing real work. Fix: add one sentence using the non-European communities as a control case. This also partly answers the Eurocentrism charge.

“Bildungstradition” as overlooked factor: I already invoke “zweitausend Jahre Textkultur.” Reviewer C’s point about Berufsverbote channelling Jews into trade/medicine/scholarship is a fair mechanism to add briefly. Accept as a sentence, not a section.

22. Eurocentrism / non-European communities (Reviewer C)#

Partly accept, partly refute. The chapter is structured around the major demographic-cultural centres, and Cochin, Kaifeng, and Beta Israel were small and (Kaifeng) extinct. They do not warrant the same treatment as Babylon or Poland — that is proportion, not bias. Refute the demand for full sub-chapters.

But accept a corrective paragraph: these communities are precisely the control cases for the Nobel argument and for the “pattern,” and a short note acknowledging them (and explaining why they remained small) improves the chapter. I’ll fold this into the conclusion rather than inflate the body.

23. “Lachrymose” bias and the over-dramatisation (A, B, C)#

Accept the stylistic correction wholeheartedly — and it matches my own preferences better than the draft does.

  • Salo Baron’s critique is exactly right: the draft reads at points as a catalogue of catastrophes. Fix: in the conclusion, make explicit that survival came through the high adaptive capacity between catastrophes — culture in the interstices — not as a miracle despite them.

  • Superlatives (“intellektuell bedeutsamste,” “dramatischste,” “vollständigste Vernichtung,” “das eigentliche Rätsel,” “bitterste Ironien”): Accept. These are precisely the kind of unearned superlatives I dislike elsewhere. Fix: cut or justify each. “das dramatischste Abbruch” also has a gender error → “der abrupteste Abbruch.”

  • Repetitions: the Nobel statistic appears in intro, excursus, and conclusion; six-million appears repeatedly. Accept. Fix: introduce the Nobel figure once (in the excursus), merely foreshadow it in the intro; give the Holocaust toll once, precisely (“rund 6 Millionen; Schätzungen 5,7–6,3 Mio., Yad Vashem”).

24. The “Staffellauf” vs. network point (Reviewer A)#

Accept — this is the best conceptual criticism in the three reviews.

The sequential “one station hands off to the next” image is contradicted by my own data: Babylon, Alexandria, and Judaea flourished simultaneously; Spain and the Rhineland overlapped. The diaspora is a network with shifting centres of gravity, and the “Ausweichbecken” generally existed before the catastrophe that drove people into them — which is itself why survival was possible.

Fix: rewrite the opening metaphor. The “Muster” is not a relay but a network in which the centre of gravity migrates; new centres are typically already established when an old one collapses. This actually strengthens the survival thesis (there was always somewhere to go) and I’ll say so.

25. Palestinian perspective / zionist narrative (Reviewer C)#

Partly accept, partly refute.

I refute the framing that the chapter “uncritically adopts a Zionist narrative.” I explicitly call Israel “historisch kontingent,” deny it any guarantee, and pose its possible end as an open question — that is not triumphalism.

I accept one concrete addition: the founding of Israel coincided with the Palestinian Nakba, and a chapter that honestly tracks “the end usually comes from outside, to minorities under changing majorities” owes the reader at least a sentence noting that the establishment of the Jewish state produced a new displaced population. One clause, factual, no editorialising: “Die Gründung Israels 1948 fiel mit der Flucht und Vertreibung von rund 700.000 Palästinensern zusammen (Nakba).”

I refute the demand to import Butler/Chomsky/Arendt as “balance.” This is a chapter on the geography and demography of Jewish communities, not on the political theory of Zionism (which the cross-references assign to other chapters). Adding named polemicists would be tribal framing of exactly the kind I avoid. Arendt could earn a footnote only where directly relevant; the others do not belong here.

26. Source-citation demands (all three)#

Accept. Demographic and Nobel figures need provenance. Fix: cite Baron and DellaPergola for demography, name the Nobel compilation and the inclusion criterion, attribute the Roman-era range to Harnack (high) vs. Schwartz (low). This is consistent with how I handle quantification elsewhere and the draft simply failed to do it.

Summary of what changes#

Substantive corrections I accept: the 2.800-year arithmetic and the diaspora definition; sovereignty dating; no total expulsion in 135; Syria Palaestina intent; rabbinic anachronism; Persian-period phrasing; Mizrachim/Sephardim; Roman numbers and “religio licita”; Yiddish-vs-Hebrew in the USSR (the worst factual slip); Ansiedlungsrayon date; Herzl’s birthplace; Baeyer (not Jewish); German-Jewish US wave; Latin American and Israeli figures; Persia “only country” error; al-Andalus romanticisation; Chmelnizki numbers; “deutsche Vernichtungslager im besetzten Polen”; the fourth Nobel factor (urbanisation/networks) and the control-case logic; source citations; the superlatives and repetitions; the Nakba clause; and — conceptually most important — the network-not-relay correction and the anti-lachrymose reframing of the conclusion.

Points I refute: Israel’s sovereignty as “disputed”; “first sovereignty since 135 CE” as an error (it is correctly since, the Hasmoneans predate it); Raschi-in-SchUM (I never claimed it); the Roman “Dhimmi” misreading; full sub-chapters for Cochin/Kaifeng/Beta Israel (handled proportionally instead); and the importation of named anti-Zionist authors as mandatory “balance.”

The reviewers have done me a service: the chapter argued for statistical scruple in the Nobel section while being cavalier with antique demography everywhere else. That inconsistency is the real defect, and most of the fixes amount to applying one standard throughout.
