I’ll work through the comments by topic, since the three reviewers overlap heavily. I’ll mark each point Accept / Refute / Partial.

Factual corrections#

Black September death toll (“Zehntausende getötet”). Accept. This is my error — the figure traces to PLO-era propaganda. Credible estimates cluster around 2,000–5,000. Reviewer A says 2,000–3,400, Reviewer B says 3,000–5,000; the honest move is a range with a marker: “Schätzungen zwischen ca. 2.000 und 5.000 Toten.” I’ll drop “Zehntausende.”

Jewish refugees: “Entschädigung und Staatsbürgerschaft”. Accept — both A and B are right and this is a genuine factual flaw that actually undercuts my own symmetry argument if left as is. They received Israeli citizenship but no compensation for property lost in Arab countries (still being claimed). Corrected: “…erhielten die israelische Staatsbürgerschaft (nicht aber eine Entschädigung für ihr in den Herkunftsländern verlorenes Eigentum).” Note this sharpens the asymmetry I’m drawing, so it helps the argument.

“Entschädigung wurde nie ernsthaft angeboten”. Accept with reformulation. Both A and B correctly point to Camp David 2000 / Clinton Parameters / Taba 2001 / Olmert 2008, where an international compensation fund was concretely on the table. The accurate claim is: the sticking point was the right of return, not compensation per se. Reformulate to “…wurde diskutiert, aber nie zum Abschluss gebracht; der eigentliche Streitpunkt war das Rückkehrrecht, nicht die Entschädigung.” This is a real improvement.

Iran Jewish community (“8.000–25.000”). Accept. The upper bound is a pre-revolution relic. Current estimates are ~8,500–9,000. I’ll narrow it and drop the misleading ceiling.

Hisbollah 2024 (Nasrallah killed, Nov 2024 ceasefire). Refute the deletion, accept the marking. Reviewer B writes “to the knowledge state up to Oct 2024 not verifiable.” That reflects his cutoff, not an error in the text. These events occurred (Nasrallah killed 27 Sept 2024; ceasefire late Nov 2024). I’ll keep them but date them precisely and flag fragility, which the text already does (“mehrfach verletzt, politisch fragil”).

Iran: “entity” vs. “zionistisches Regime”. Partial. Both phrasings are used by Tehran (“zionist entity” and “zionist regime” both appear in official translations). “Regime” is the more common rendering. I’ll switch to “zionistisches Regime/Gebilde” to be unimpeachable. Minor.

Yarmouk status. Accept. Reviewer B is correct: Yarmouk is an unofficial camp, not one of the 58 official UNRWA camps. As written it implies otherwise. Fix: describe it as “der größte palästinensische Flüchtlingsbezirk (kein offizielles UNRWA-Camp)” and keep it out of the 58-count.

UNRWA outside camps (“fehlt der UNRWA-Schutz”). Accept. Genuinely misleading — UNRWA runs schools and clinics for registered refugees regardless of camp residence. The point I wanted was about legal/statelessness status, not UNRWA services. Reformulate: “Die Lager bündeln UNRWA-Infrastruktur; außerhalb ist der Zugang oft schwieriger, und wer staatenlos ist, bleibt staatenlos.”

UNHCR vs. UNRWA mandate / inherited status (A’s suggestion + C’s patrilineal point). Accept the mandate explanation; refute the patrilineal claim as stated. A’s point is good and explanatory — one half-sentence on why UNRWA status persists where UNHCR status lapses on naturalisation explains the 700k→5.9M dynamic. I’ll add it. But Reviewer C’s “nur an Nachkommen männlicher Linie / patrilinear” is wrong as a hard claim: UNRWA eligibility passes through the registered refugee and descendants of male registered refugees by the registration convention, but this is an administrative registration rule with exceptions, not the clean patrilineal law C asserts. I’ll mention the descent-based registration without overstating it.

Absentee Property Law: “Es gab keine Entschädigung”. Accept (soften). B is right — there were limited, mostly token compensation schemes later (1953 land acquisition law, minor payments) that changed little. “weitgehend ohne Entschädigung” is more accurate.

East Jerusalem annexation (C). Accept. Trivial fix — name Ostjerusalem explicitly at first mention; I already mention it later.

Annexation not internationally recognised (B). Accept. One clause: the 1950 annexation was recognised only by Britain (and Pakistan), otherwise rejected. Useful context.

All-Palestine passports issuer (B). Accept. Precise: issued by the All-Palestine Government under Egyptian protection. Minor.

7 October hostage count. Accept. “rund 250” is more robust than the false precision of 251. Fine.

Flüchtlingszahl 726.000 (C). Refute. The UNCC figure of 726,000 is itself an estimate within the scholarly range; presenting it as “more precise than your interval” is false precision. Morris and others give ranges precisely because the number is uncertain. I keep the range; I can cite 726,000 as the UNCC midpoint, but I won’t pretend it’s exact.

Hisbollah founding — IRGC presence from 1981 (C). Accept (already half-present). The text says the Iran sent Revolutionary Guards and that 1982 was the “Katalysator.” C’s point refines the timing; I’ll note IRGC presence preceded and the invasion catalysed. Consistent with what I wrote.

Iran–Israel pre-1979 “offiziell” (C). Partial accept. Relations were close and substantial but Iran never extended de jure recognition (Project Flower etc. were secret). I’ll change “diplomatische Beziehungen” to “de-facto-diplomatische Beziehungen” — the Shah maintained a de facto mission. Not “official.”

Logical / analytical points#

Selection bias in “endet selten durch militärischen Sieg”. Accept — this is the best single criticism. Both A and C are right: LTTE (Sri Lanka 2009), Chechnya, and others were militarily crushed. My pattern is selected on survivors. I’ll reformulate honestly: armed resistance by weaker groups has two terminal modes — political settlement, or military destruction (LTTE, Chechnya). Which one obtains is not predetermined. This strengthens the chapter precisely because it removes a determinism I’d otherwise have to defend. Note that conceding this slightly weakens the implied optimism of my conclusion (“diese Logik gilt auch hier”) — I’ll keep that as a possibility, not a law.

Determinism of the central causal chain (all three). Accept (calibrate, don’t gut). The chapter already hedges (“keine Rechtfertigung,” “es bedeutet nicht, dass Terrorismus unvermeidlich ist,” the nonviolence paragraph). But B and C are right that I should name covariates explicitly: regional ideologies, external sponsors (Iran), host-state repression, intra-Palestinian politics. I’ll add one sentence making these explicit cofactors and change “trieben zur Gewalt” to “erhöhten die Wahrscheinlichkeit, ohne sie zu determinieren.” That phrasing is exactly my register.

Israeli “Kalkül” re Hamas/Mujama (A and B). Accept (de-conspiratorialise). Both reviewers are right and consistent. What is documented: Israel tolerated and legalised Mujama al-Islamiya, a then-charitable Islamist network, as a counterweight to the secular PLO; the militant turn came with the First Intifada (1987). Targeted “promotion of an armed group” is not documented and I shouldn’t imply it. I’ll change “tolerierte und begünstigte” → “duldete und indirekt stärkte,” and add the clause that this was a charitable/da’wa network, not a militia, at the time. Keeps the “Fehlkalkulation” point, removes the conspiracy smell.

Naksa 1967 + settlements + Oslo as radicalisation factors (C). Accept partially. C is right that 1967 (≈300,000 newly displaced) and post-1967 settlements explain why the camps became permanent and why hope of return eroded — this is causally central and currently missing. I’ll add a short paragraph “Warum die Lager dauerhaft wurden” covering 1967 and settlement expansion. I’ll decline to load the Oslo-settlements claim heavily here; it belongs in a chapter on the peace process, and overstating it would itself be a contested causal claim.

UNO / superpower blockade as a responsible party (C). Partial. Cold-War superpower obstruction is real but C’s framing (“USA/UDSSR verhinderten eine Regelung um Einfluss zu behalten”) is a strong causal claim that needs more than assertion. I’ll add the international community’s Cold-War paralysis as a factor under “internationale Gemeinschaft,” without the monocausal “to keep influence” gloss.

Jordan integrated only in West Bank, not East Bank (C). Refute as stated. This is misleading. Jordan’s 1954 citizenship law granted citizenship to Palestinians on both banks; the discrimination C refers to is real but is post-1970 (Black September) and social/political, not a denial of citizenship. I won’t rewrite the text to suggest Jordan withheld East Bank citizenship — it didn’t. I can add that Jordanian citizenship did not mean full social equality.

Style#

“Eines der grausamsten juristischen Konstrukte”. Accept. A and B both flag it; consistent with my own stated dislike of moralising adjectives. “folgenreichsten und umstrittensten” is better and harder to attack. The bureaucratic coldness does more work than “grausam.”

“darf nicht in einem Atemzug genannt werden”. Accept. A is right — it’s a command to the reader, which I dislike when others do it. Replace with the analytic contrast: “Im Gegensatz zu PLO, Hamas und Hisbollah ist der Iran ein souveräner Nationalstaat…”

“bedeutendste Macht”. Accept. Superlative; I disapprove of these on principle. “eine der einflussreichsten, weder arabisch noch türkisch” is accurate.

“strategische Fehlkalkulation von historischem Ausmaß”, “Laboratorium für die Erzeugung extremer Positionen”, “vollständig marginalisiert”. Accept the dampening. “von historischem Ausmaß” → cut. “Laboratorium” → I’ll keep one controlled metaphor but anchor it in concrete conditions (overcrowding, work bans, no prospects) as C suggests, rather than leaving it abstract. “vollständig marginalisiert” → “weitgehend” (Shatila residents do have some rights). “Brutkasten/Ghetto” (C’s suggestion) — refute: “Ghetto” imports a loaded analogy I don’t want; I’ll avoid it.

Repetition of “Lager” (25×) / “Staatenlosigkeit” vs “keine Staatsbürgerschaft” (C). Partial. I’ll vary with “Flüchtlingssiedlungen,” “permanente Provisorien” where natural, and pick one of stateless/citizenship per passage. I decline “Ghetto” (see above). Mild edit; I won’t manufacture variation that reduces precision.

“Die Frage ist nicht… Die Frage ist…” too woodcut (C). Refute (keep). This is a deliberate rhetorical hinge that separates the moral question (is it justified? no) from the causal one (who created the conditions?). That distinction is the spine of the chapter. I’ll keep it but add the agency point C wants nearby, so it doesn’t read as erasing choice.

Iran section length#

Cut the Iran section / outsource it (C, strongly). Refute, mostly. Reviewers B and A treat the Iran section as substantive and only ask for factual fixes; C alone wants it cut. The Iran material does real work: the “Achse des Widerstands” is why the refugee-driven groups (Hamas, Hisbollah) became regionally weaponised — that’s a covariate the same reviewer C demanded I make explicit elsewhere. I’ll trim the nuclear-technology detail (C and the JCPOA mechanics can compress to two sentences — accept that) but keep the structural analysis. Cutting it would remove exactly the external-sponsor factor needed to de-determinise the central causal chain.

Bias / balance#

Bibliography tilts to New Historians (B, C). Accept. Fair. Khalidi/Masalha/Shlaim/Morris is not balanced enough. I’ll add Karsh and Gelber (and possibly Shapira) as counterweight, plus UNRWA primary data. Morris is already cited and is himself contested terrain — I’ll note the “partly planned, partly chaos” thesis explicitly, which strengthens neutrality.

Mizrahim integration “letztlich erfolgreich” oversimplified (C). Accept. Right — integration was real but accompanied by lasting Mizrahi discrimination. I already gesture at “social tensions (Kapitel 6)”; I’ll make the asymmetry C identifies explicit: Palestinians were actively prevented (law, military) from integrating; Mizrahim were integrated but discriminated against. That difference matters and helps the symmetry argument by being honest about its limits.

UNRWA presented uncritically; Hamas infiltration; 2024 funding cuts (C). Partial accept. The 2024 funding suspensions (US, Germany, others after the January 2024 allegations, several later restored) are relevant and current — I’ll add a sentence. On “Hamas-Infiltration … UN Watch reports”: refute as a sourcing recommendation. UN Watch is an advocacy organisation, not a neutral source; I won’t cite it as authority. I can note neutrally that UNRWA faces allegations of staff links to Hamas and that these are contested and under investigation, which is the defensible formulation.

Structure of the resistance analogies#

Résistance/FLN/IRA “not comparable” (C). Refute, partially. C’s objection — Résistance fought an occupier, Palestinians fight a state — assumes the answer to the very dispute the chapter is about (occupation vs. sovereign territory). I won’t smuggle that conclusion in. The analogies illustrate the general mechanism (asymmetric armed resistance, reprisal dynamics, eventual political resolution), not legal equivalence, and I say so. I’ll accept adding LTTE and ANC — ANC is already in §6, and LTTE I’m adding anyway as the counterexample to the no-military-victory claim. So C’s constructive half lands; the “incomparable” half I reject.

10:1 reprisals “not uniform doctrine” (B). Accept. Minor precision: “oft” / “teils” rather than implying a single fixed ratio. The 10:1 was a frequent but not universal German practice.

Huthi vs Houthi (B). Accept. German convention, trivial.

Net#

I accept the bulk of the factual corrections — several (Jewish-refugee compensation, the compensation-was-offered point, the selection-bias on liberation movements, the Mizrahi asymmetry) actually sharpen the argument rather than soften it, which is the useful kind of criticism. I’m dampening the handful of moralising adjectives and the one command to the reader, consistent with my own standards. I decline: cutting the Iran section to a stub, the “incomparable analogies” objection, citing UN Watch, the “Ghetto” wording, the false-precision 726,000, and the two Jordan/UNRWA claims (East Bank citizenship, strict patrilineality) that are themselves inaccurate.
