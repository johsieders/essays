Below I go through every distinct point. I group them and mark each ACCEPT or REFUTE with the action or the reason. Where reviewers overlap, I treat the point once.

1. Factual corrections#

SchUM = Schpira, Warmaisa, Magenza (not “Usch”) — A and B. ACCEPT. Clear error. The U/ש-ו-ם stands for Warmaisa (Worms). Fix to: “Schpira (Speyer), Warmaisa (Worms), Magenza (Mainz)”. This is the most embarrassing slip in the chapter; correct unconditionally.

Raschi wirkte in Troyes, studierte in Worms/Mainz — B. ACCEPT. My text implies Raschi worked in the SchUM towns. He studied there but worked in Troyes. Reformulate: “Aus diesen Akademien ging Raschi hervor, der in Worms und Mainz studierte und später in Troyes wirkte.” His Old-French glosses fit Troyes anyway, so the correction strengthens the passage.

Patriarchat abolished 429, not 425 — B. ACCEPT. Death of Gamaliel VI under Theodosius II is dated to c. 425–429; the standard date for the legal end is 429. I will write “um 429” to absorb the uncertainty.

Caesarea is not Galilee — B. ACCEPT. Caesarea Maritima is coastal, not Galilean. Replace with Usha and Beit Sche’arim (both genuine rabbinic centres), keeping Tiberias and Sepphoris.

Yerushalmi 4.–5. Jh.; Bavli 6.–frühes 7. Jh. — B. ACCEPT (minor). “um 500–600” is defensible but I will write “5./6. Jahrhundert, mit späteren Redaktionen bis ins frühe 7.” for the Bavli and “um 400” for the Yerushalmi. No substantive change to the argument.

Torquemada “konvertierter Jude in zweiter Generation” — B. ACCEPT. The converso-ancestry claim rests on a single hostile later source (Pulgar) and is largely rejected. The “personal obsession” reading depends on it. I will delete both: “Der Generalinquisitor Tomás de Torquemada verfolgte die Conversos mit großer Härte.” Loses nothing the argument needs.

Portugal 1497: Zwangstaufen, nicht Vertreibung — B. ACCEPT. Manuel I formally ordered expulsion (1496) but then prevented departure and forcibly baptised most Jews in 1497. “vertrieb sie fünf Jahre später ebenfalls” is wrong. Rewrite: “Portugal nahm zunächst welche auf, zwang sie aber 1497 unter Manuel I. überwiegend zur Taufe und verhinderte faktisch die Ausreise.”

“Im selben Monat” (1492 Kolumbus) — A (implicitly via “same month”), B. ACCEPT. Edict 31 March, deadline end of July/2 August, Columbus sailed 3 August. The month claim is false; the year is the real coincidence. I used “im selben Monat” twice (intro and §8) and “in denselben Wochen” once. I will standardise to “im selben Jahr”, and where I want the sharper image keep the defensible version: “in derselben Woche, in der die Frist ablief, stach Kolumbus in See.” That is literally true and keeps the rhetorical punch — the kind of move Russell would approve of, since it is both surprising and correct.

Matrilinearity and the Law of Return — B. ACCEPT. This is a real error. The Law of Return (1950, amended 1970) admits a child or grandchild of a Jew and their spouses — explicitly patrilineal and broader than Halacha. The matrilineal rule governs orthodox recognition (marriage, the Rabbinate), not immigration eligibility. Fix: remove “wer nach dem israelischen Rückkehrgesetz einwandern darf” from the matrilineal consequences and state the actual conflict — Law of Return is generous, the Rabbinate is strict, and the gap between them is itself the source of friction.

Shmuel ha-Nagid “einziger Jude der gesamten Diaspora, der je eine nichtjüdische Armee befehligte” — B. ACCEPT. Refuted by John Monash (Australian corps commander, 1918), among others. My absolute claim is false. Restrict to: “im Mittelalter ein Einzelfall — jüdischer Wesir und Feldherr eines muslimischen Staates.” This is the formulation B suggests and it is defensible.

Karäer “bis zu 40%” — A (genesis), B, C. ACCEPT. The 40% figure traces to polemical rabbinic sources (ibn Daud) dramatising the threat. Daniel Frank’s work supports 10–20% in core regions at most. Replace with: “In einigen Regionen (vor allem Byzanz und Ägypten) erreichten sie zeitweise erhebliche Anteile — genug, um als ernsthafte Konkurrenz wahrgenommen zu werden.” I drop the spurious precision rather than swap one contested number for another.

Karäer genesis: Anan founded the Ananites, Karaism crystallised 9th–10th c. — A. ACCEPT (nuance). Fair. Anan was retrospectively made founding father; biblicist Karaism proper formed later under Mutazilite influence. I will add one clause: “Anan gilt als Gründervater, doch der eigentliche Karäismus formierte sich erst im 9./10. Jahrhundert aus mehreren antirabbinischen Strömungen.” Keeps it brief.

Bayezid quote is legend — B (already flagged in my text), C. PARTLY ACCEPT. I already mark it as “erst in späteren jüdischen Quellen … nicht zweifelsfrei belegt” and assert only the documented fact (ships were sent). C wants me to replace it with a pragmatic-policy sentence. I keep the quote — flagged as legend it is good narrative and I have already discharged the honesty obligation — but I will add C’s substantive point: Ottoman policy was pragmatic (tax base, skilled artisans, including in artillery and printing). That adds content without removing colour.

Geniza: Solomon Schechter fehlt — A. ACCEPT. Genuine gap. Schechter cleared the Geniza in 1896–97 and brought ~190,000 fragments to Cambridge; Goitein worked that corpus from the 1950s. I will insert one sentence: “1896 räumte Solomon Schechter die Geniza und brachte rund 190.000 Fragmente nach Cambridge; auf diesem Bestand baute Goitein ab den 1950ern auf.” Without Schechter the chain is incomplete.

Population: relation to Reich population was 3–7%, not 10%; Baron/Mommsen provenance — C. ACCEPT. The downward revision affects the percentage too. I will add: “Die Revision betrifft auch den Anteil an der Reichsbevölkerung — eher 3–7% als die früher genannten 10%.” Attributing the 8-million figure to Baron is correct and I can name him in one clause. I will not over-elaborate the historiography.

Einstein “als die Nazis ihn verfolgten” — ungenau — C. PARTLY ACCEPT. C is right that the mechanism in 1933 was dismissal of “non-Aryan” officials, predating the Nuremberg Laws. But my sentence is about identity ascription, not legal chronology, and “verfolgten” is not false — his property was seized, a bounty was placed on him, he never returned. I will tighten to: “Als das NS-Regime ihn 1933 zum Feind erklärte und sein Vermögen einzog, …” — accurate without implying a specific legal instrument. On the Einstein placement (A): see structural section below. On C’s exact 1921 quote: optional improvement; I will use it, since the real wording (“kein Priesterkaste … frei von anthropomorphen Zügen”) is sharper than my paraphrase and ties to the Maimonides passage on the non-anthropomorphic God. Nice internal echo.

2. Logical / comparative points#

Comparison with Phoenicians, Philistines, Moabites, Edomites is chronologically skewed — A. ACCEPT. A is right: those peoples disappeared in the Iron Age under Assyrian/Babylonian/Persian pressure, centuries before 135 CE and under different conditions. The comparison I want is “peoples that lost territory and assimilated” generally, but the framing “unter ähnlichen Bedingungen” overclaims. Two options: (a) keep these peoples but drop “ähnliche Bedingungen” and say the contrast is the absence of a portable textual core; (b) add Romanised cases (Gauls, Carthaginians, Nabataeans). I will do (a) and add the Nabataeans as one chronologically closer case. The point survives; the sloppy “ähnliche Bedingungen” goes.

“1.800 Jahre Diaspora” jars with a chapter ending in 1492 — B. ACCEPT. One clarifying clause: the 1,800 years is the whole arc to the 20th century; this chapter covers 135–1492. Cheap to fix, removes a real reader stumble.

“ohne politische Souveränität” vs. the Khazars — B. ACCEPT. Internal tension. Fix the opening: “ohne eigene politische Souveränität (von seltenen Ausnahmen wie den Chasaren abgesehen)”. One parenthesis resolves it.

Matrilinearity causation: Roman ius gentium more likely than the rape hypothesis — A; B asks to mark it as hypothesis. ACCEPT. A’s substantive point (Shaye Cohen) is the stronger scholarly position: under Roman law the offspring of an invalid union follows the mother. My rape-after-Bar-Kochba story is folk-etymology-grade. I will lead with the legal-pragmatic explanation (mother is certain; assimilation of the Roman parallel) and demote the rape hypothesis to “auch dies wurde vermutet, ist aber spekulativ” — or cut it. Cohen also notes the differing treatment of rape-offspring, which undercuts my version directly. Good catch.

Endogamy vs. open conversion (Khazars) — unresolved tension — C. ACCEPT (small). One sentence resolves it: endogamy operated within established communities; conversion, when it happened, was typically collective or individual entry that enlarged the community without dissolving its boundary rules. Worth adding.

Why was the text more effective than the Avesta? — C. PARTLY ACCEPT. The distinction — Torah as life-regulating law, not merely cultic — is exactly the bridge to my own “Halacha als Verfassung” section, so it is already implicit. I will make it explicit with one comparative clause (Zoroastrian Avesta as primarily cultic). I will not expand into a comparative-religion excursus.

Messianism’s political dimension (Bar Kochba, Sabbatai Zwi) — C. PARTLY ACCEPT. Worth one clause noting that messianic energy could turn political (Bar Kochba already opens the chapter; Sabbatai Zwi I already name). I will add that the same hope could become political mobilisation, which also sets up the later “Zionism as secularised messianism” line. No major addition.

Dhimmi ambivalence / parallel to Sicut Judaeis — C. ACCEPT (partly). The parallel between Muslim dhimma and the papal Sicut Judaeis tradition of “protected but subordinate” is apt and reinforces my thesis that neither side was monolithic. I will add a short comparative sentence. I will not restructure the Dhimmi passage into three subsections (C’s suggestion) — see style.

3. Bias: Islam-too-positive / Christianity-too-negative#

General charge — C. PARTLY REFUTE, PARTLY ACCEPT. The asymmetry in the chapter is not (only) authorial bias; it reflects a real asymmetry in outcomes, and I already include the counterweights: Almohad persecution and Maimonides’ flight, the Mohammed-vs-Jewish-tribes bloodshed, the explicit statement “Die islamische Welt war nicht immer tolerant”, and Bernard Lewis’s “second-class citizens” (not “equal”). On the Christian side I already note bishops sheltering Jews, popes denouncing the blood libel and the plague accusation, Carolingian favour, and Norwich. So the dichotomy is less schematic than C implies.

That said, two concrete additions improve balance cheaply and I accept them: a clause that Otto I and some emperors actively protected Jews in the HRR, and the Sicut Judaeis protection tradition. I will not add “Abd al-Malik destroyed synagogues in Jerusalem” — that claim is weakly sourced and would import a new factual liability to fix a stylistic complaint.

I refute the implied remedy of mechanical symmetry. The honest summary is Lewis’s: under Islam Jews were subordinate citizens; in Christian Europe, frequently non-citizens subject to expulsion. Flattening that into “both had tolerant and fanatic phases” would be false balance. I keep the asymmetry but soften the absolutes (see style).

Sephardim-open / Aschkenasim-narrow is a cliché — C. ACCEPT. Fair. Joseph Karo (Sephardi, ultra-halachic Schulchan Aruch) and the Tosafists (Ashkenazi scholastic innovators) cut against the stereotype, and the Haskala originated in Ashkenazi Berlin. I will add “Die Unterschiede waren graduell, nicht absolut” and one example each direction. I had already written “weniger fokussiert … neigte zu”, i.e. comparative not absolute, but the qualifier should be explicit.

4. Missing contexts#

Radhanites — C. ACCEPT (qualified). Worth one sentence as an example of long-distance Jewish trade networks. But REFUTE C’s specific placement and sourcing: the Radhanites are attested mainly by ibn Khordadbeh (9th c.), not primarily by the Geniza, and “Christiania” is an anachronism (founded 1624). I will add them in the early-medieval trade context (§5, Carolingian) with correct attribution, not as a Geniza finding.

Jewish women / Geniza — C. PARTLY ACCEPT. The Geniza does illuminate women’s lives and I can add one concrete sentence (the Genizah’s marriage contracts, businesswomen, letters). REFUTE the specific examples as offered: “Sitt al-Mulk” was the Fatimid regent, not a Geniza merchant woman, and “Dona Vandale” I cannot verify — I will not insert unverified names. I will make the point generically and accurately rather than with dubious specifics. The matrilinearity-as-women’s-rights framing (C) I refuse: it is anachronistic and the matrilineal rule is about status transmission, not women’s legal protection.

Comparison to Armenians, Roma — C. REFUTE (as scope creep). A worthwhile idea but it belongs in a comparative conclusion, not retrofitted here. The chapter is already long. I note it as a candidate for the book’s final synthesis, not this chapter.

5. Style and absolutes#

“Mikrostaat im fremden Staat” — A, C. PARTLY ACCEPT. A is technically right (a state implies a monopoly on force, which the Kehilla lacked; authority was delegated and revocable). But “Mikrostaat” is a deliberate metaphor and I immediately qualify it: “schwach, abhängig, jederzeit angreifbar”. I will keep the metaphor but add “ohne Gewaltmonopol, jede Befugnis vom Herrscher delegiert und widerrufbar” so the limit is explicit. I will not replace it with “Körperschaft des öffentlichen Rechts avant la lettre” — that is precise and dead.

Soften absolutes: “ohne Parallele”, “einzig”, “atemberaubend”, “Weltliteratur”, “kategorialer Unterschied” — B. ACCEPT. This matches my own stylistic preferences (the dislike of superlatives is, after all, mine). Specific changes: “ohne Parallele” → “in dieser Form kaum vergleichbar”; “von atemberaubender Dimension” → “von außerordentlicher Reichweite”; “gehören zur Weltliteratur” → “werden zur Weltliteratur gezählt”; Lewis’s “kategorialer Unterschied, keine Gradfrage” → “ein grundlegender, kein gradueller Unterschied”. I keep “einzigartige Quelle” for the Geniza — there C correctly notes it is factually accurate (no comparable corpus exists), so the superlative is earned in Russell’s sense.

Inquisition “überstieg die Grausamkeit der meisten zeitgenössischen Gerichte” — B. ACCEPT. Contested and overstated; secular courts of the era were often as cruel, and the Inquisition’s procedures were in some respects more formalised. Rewrite: “setzte systematisch Folter und drakonische Strafen ein und war in ihrer Wirkung außergewöhnlich nachhaltig.” Accurate, less polemical.

Chassidismus “gegen die talmudische Scholastik” — wertend — B. ACCEPT. Reformulate neutrally: “als pietistische Erneuerungsbewegung, in Reaktion auf soziale Krisen und eine als formalistisch empfundene Gelehrsamkeit.” The word “empfunden” carries the load honestly.

Dhimmi-Bauhöhe, Pest-Hygiene as categorical claims — B. ACCEPT both. “keine Moscheen überragen” → “ihre Bauten durften, je nach Ort und Zeit, vorgeschriebene Höhen nicht überschreiten”. Plague/hygiene: this is genuinely speculative; I will mark it: “gelegentlich wird geringere Betroffenheit durch rituelle Hygiene vermutet, doch belegt ist das nicht.”

Strasbourg 1349 numbers — B. ACCEPT. Estimates vary (often ~900, sometimes up to 2,000). I will write “mehrere hundert bis etwa 2.000” rather than assert 2,000. England 1290 “etwa 3.000” I leave; it is within the accepted range.

Long paragraphs, “Goldenes Zeitalter” repeated 5×, “außergewöhnliche Quelle”, “traumatische Erinnerung” — C. PARTLY ACCEPT. I will vary “Goldenes Zeitalter” where it is mere repetition (Blütezeit, Periode der Gunst) but keep it where it is the established term being discussed. “traumatische Erinnerung” → “nachhaltige Prägung der jüdischen Erinnerung”. I refute the call to split Dhimmi/Expulsion into labelled subsections and to add tables, maps, and a comparison grid: this is a narrative-essay chapter, not a textbook, and the table would force exactly the schematic Islam/Christianity opposition C elsewhere (correctly) warns against. One clean paragraph that states the asymmetry and its limits is better than a 2×4 grid that hard-codes it.

Structural: the Einstein excursus#

A and B/C all touch this. ACCEPT. Einstein in a chapter ending in 1492 breaks the chronology — A is right. But §7 (“Identität ohne Territorium”) is a deliberately synchronic, thematic coda, not part of the 135–1492 narrative. The fix is to frame it explicitly as a forward-looking thematic section (“ein Vorgriff, der den Bogen schlägt”), and to move the Einstein illustration’s heavier machinery (1933, Zionism, the Nazi bounty) into a one-paragraph signpost pointing to the modern chapter, keeping here only the minimal “secular Jew is still a Jew” point with the 1921 quote. That preserves the conceptual payload (identity without religious content, which I need now to close the survival argument) while not pretending Einstein belongs in 1492.

Summary of disposition#

  • Accepted outright (factual): SchUM, Raschi/Troyes, Nasi 429, Caesarea→Usha/Beit Sche’arim, Talmud datings, Torquemada, Portugal 1497, 1492 calendar, Law of Return, ha-Nagid uniqueness, Karäer 40%→qualitative, Karaism genesis, Schechter, population %, Einstein 1933 wording.

  • Accepted (logical/structural): Phoenician comparison, 1,800-year framing, Khazar/sovereignty tension, matrilinearity causation, endogamy/conversion, Avesta contrast, Sephardi/Ashkenazi cliché, Einstein framing.

  • Accepted (style): all the absolute-softening, Inquisition, Chassidismus, dhimmi-height, plague-hygiene, Strasbourg numbers, “Mikrostaat” qualifier, repetition.

  • Partly accepted: Bayezid (keep quote + add policy), bias charge (add Otto I + Sicut Judaeis, keep the real asymmetry), women (generic accurate mention, reject the unverified names and the anachronistic rights framing), Radhanites (add with correct source/placement, reject Geniza attribution and “Christiania”).

  • Refuted: mechanical Islam/Christianity symmetry and false balance; tables/maps/grid; Abd al-Malik synagogue claim; Armenian/Roma comparison in this chapter; “Sitt al-Mulk”/”Dona Vandale”; matrilinearity-as-women’s-rights; replacing “Mikrostaat” and “einzigartige Quelle” with flatter terms.

The two reviewers worth their fee are A and B: dense, specific, every claim checkable. C is generous with content but pays for breadth with several unverified specifics — exactly the failure mode one should fix in others’ work and dread in one’s own.
