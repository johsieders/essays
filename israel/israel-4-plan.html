Below I work through each substantive point. I group them to avoid repetition, since the three reviewers overlap heavily.

1. Structure: Section 8 (Christianity and Judaism)#

Reviewer A wants it cut drastically, ending at “the silence of the churches (1945)”; the post-1965 material to migrate to a later chapter.

Partly accept, partly refute.

Reviewer A is right that the section breaks the chronological frame and the dramaturgical arc toward 1948. My disclaimer concedes as much — and a disclaimer that says “this doesn’t belong here, skip it if you like” is an admission that the editing job is unfinished. One does not apologise in print for one’s own table of contents.

But the remedy is not to amputate at 1945. The reason the section exists is the causal claim in its first paragraph: the Holocaust “stood at the end of a nearly two-thousand-year tradition of Christian hostility.” That claim earns its place in this chapter because it explains the soil. The Augustinian Zeugnislehre, Luther’s 1543 pamphlet, the perfidis Judaeis prayer — all pre-1948, all directly relevant to “how was this possible.”

What does not belong here is the post-war reconciliation narrative: Nostra Aetate (1965), the Ratzinger episode (2008/2018), Franziskus. That is a 60-year arc about the aftermath, and Reviewer A is correct that it belongs in a thematic chapter on the long-term effects of the Shoah.

Fix: Keep the theological Vorgeschichte (Augustine → Luther → liturgy → the silence of the churches 1933–1945) in this chapter, as the closing analytical movement before the Schluss. Move the entire post-1965 reconciliation arc (Nostra Aetate, the consequences 1974–2015, the Benedikt episode, the limits of reconciliation) to a later chapter. This also resolves a side benefit: many of the other comments on Section 8 (Reviewer C’s protestant-perspective and arabic-Christian points, Reviewer A’s “nett zu uns Juden” wording) then apply to that chapter, not this one, and I address them there.

This is the single largest structural change and I accept its spirit while declining the specific cut line.

2. Factual corrections#

Karski / Churchill (Reviewer B). Accept. Karski met Eden (1943) and Roosevelt (July 1943) personally; no reliable record of a personal Churchill meeting exists. Change to: “berichtete der britischen und amerikanischen Führung — Eden 1943, Roosevelt persönlich im Juli 1943.”

Kennzeichnungs-Chronologie (Reviewer B). Accept. My text lets “erzwungene Kennzeichnung” sit right after the 1938 pogrom, which falsely implies the yellow star was a 1938 measure. The star became compulsory in the Reich in September 1941. The 1938/39 steps were the “J” stamp in passports and the forced middle names “Israel/Sara.” Fix: “Danach folgten Zwangsverkauf jüdischer Betriebe (Arisierung), Berufsverbote in fast allen Bereichen, der ‘J’-Stempel und Namenszwang 1938/39; die Kennzeichnung mit dem gelben Stern wurde im Reichsgebiet erst im September 1941 verpflichtend.” This is one of Reviewer B’s three priority items and it is a clean correction.

Voting behaviour of German Jews 1932 (both A’s silence and Reviewer B). Accept. The clause “Minderheiten die KPD oder das Zentrum” is wrong and slightly absurd — the Zentrum was a Catholic party. Jewish votes concentrated on DDP/DStP (Deutsche Staatspartei) and SPD, with little for KPD and essentially none for DNVP/NSDAP. Fix: “Die meisten deutschen Juden wählten 1932 liberal-bürgerlich (DDP/DStP) und sozialdemokratisch, kaum rechtsnational oder nationalsozialistisch.”

Bombing of Auschwitz: “blieben ungetroffen” (Reviewer B, Reviewer C). Accept the precision. There were stray/collateral hits in the complex during the 1944 raids on the IG-Farben/Monowitz plant; the crematoria and ramps were not deliberately targeted. Fix: “Die Rampen und Krematorien wurden nicht gezielt angegriffen; es gab 1944 vereinzelte Kollateraltreffer im Lagerkomplex.”

Emigration figures: Germany incl./excl. Austria (Reviewer B). Accept. I should state the base. The 280,000–300,000 refers to Reich Jews (Altreich); after the Anschluss a further ~120,000–130,000 Austrian Jews emigrated. Fix: add “(aus dem Altreich; hinzu kamen nach dem ‘Anschluss’ rund 120.000 österreichische Juden).” This also makes the table note (point 3 below) consistent.

Denmark numbers (Reviewer B). Accept as optional precision. My “über 7.200 … nur etwa 500” is roughly right; I’ll tighten to “rund 7.200 rechtzeitig nach Schweden gerettet; 472 deportiert, meist nach Theresienstadt.” Minor, but free accuracy.

Kielce / Polish post-war murders: “1.000–2.000” (Reviewer C). Partly accept. Reviewer C says current estimates are ~500–1,000 total for 1944–1947 and that my figure is too high. The literature genuinely varies: older estimates (Engel) range up to ~1,500–2,000; some recent work is lower. Rather than adjudicate a disputed number with a false air of precision, I’ll widen and hedge honestly: “Schätzungen reichen von mehreren hundert bis über tausend ermordeten jüdischen Rückkehrern; die Quellenlage ist umstritten.” That is more defensible than either point estimate. I do not accept replacing the number with a vague “Hunderte,” which understates the upper end of serious estimates.

3. Statistical / table clarity#

Germany 240,000 vs. 500,000 discrepancy (Reviewer A); table base year (Reviewer B). Accept both. Add a table note: “Die Bevölkerungszahlen beziehen sich auf das Jahr 1939 in den damaligen Grenzen (Deutschland ohne Österreich), nach der ersten großen Emigrationswelle seit 1933.” This single note resolves the 500k/240k puzzle and the general “what territory, what year” question.

Soviet 1.1 million — definitional dependence on 1939 vs. 1941 borders (Reviewer B). Accept a short caveat. Add a half-sentence: the figure depends strongly on whether the 1939–41 annexed territories (eastern Poland, the Baltics) are counted as “Soviet.” A reader who notices that Polish and Soviet totals must partition those territories deserves the warning.

4. Style: adjectives and moralising#

This is where the reviewers are most useful and most aligned with my own stated preferences, so I accept most of it — but not all. The rule is not “delete every adjective”; it is “let the fact carry the verdict, and keep the adjective only if it adds information the fact does not.”

“beschämendes Scheitern” (Évian) — A and B. Accept. Cut “beschämend.” Thirty-two states declining to raise quotas is the verdict. “…war ein Scheitern: 32 Länder erhöhten ihre Quoten nicht.”

“bemerkenswertes Dokument” (Wannsee) — A. Accept. Reviewer A’s replacement is better because it is informative: “Das Protokoll ist von einer bürokratischen Kälte geprägt, mit der die Ermordung von Millionen wie ein Verwaltungsproblem behandelt wird.” “Kälte” tells the reader something; “bemerkenswert” tells him nothing.

“grausame Frage” (Eichmann) — A. Refute, with a smaller fix. Reviewer A wants “historisch unsensible” or “unangemessene.” I decline. “Unsensibel” is the vocabulary of a diversity seminar, not of this book, and it is weaker than the thing it describes. But the adjective is doing work the question itself already does: “Warum habt ihr euch nicht gewehrt?” — asked of Auschwitz survivors — needs no label. Fix: drop the adjective entirely and let the question stand: “…Fragen, die sie in Israel hörten: ‘Warum habt ihr euch nicht gewehrt?’ — gestellt von Menschen, die nicht verstanden, was Auschwitz bedeutete.” That is more in the spirit of my own apricot rules than Reviewer A’s substitution.

“endgültig delegitimierte” (Exodus / British policy) — B. Accept the softening. “untergrub die Legitimität der britischen Mandatspolitik” is more accurate; one ship did not finally delegitimise a policy, it eroded its standing. And Reviewer C’s parallel complaint that “ging um die Welt” is vague is fair: “Die Berichte über die Exodus 1947 lösten internationale Empörung aus und untergruben die britische Mandatspolitik.” One revision serves both comments.

“Die Bilanz ist ernüchternd” (churches) — B. Moot for this chapter under point 1 (moves to the later chapter); I’ll apply “die Gesamtbilanz bleibt defizitär” there.

“wie Schafe zur Schlachtbank … ist falsch” → “unzutreffend” (B). Refute. This is over-correction. “Falsch” is a plain, factual word, not a moralising one. A claim about whether Jews resisted is either true or false, and it is false. “Unzutreffend” is bureaucratic softening that buys nothing. I keep “falsch.”

“Legitimationsbeschleuniger” neologism — B. Accept partially. I like the word and will keep it, but define it once on first use: “ein Legitimationsbeschleuniger — er beschleunigte die internationale Akzeptanz eines jüdischen Staates.” After that the term carries itself.

5. The closing sentence (“Never again”)#

A calls it a rhetorical wagging finger; B calls the “Fundament” image teleological.

Accept the substance, with my own wording. Reviewer A is right that “ob er auch für andere gilt” smuggles in a verdict — it implies the answer is no and frames the whole conflict as one side’s failure of universalisation. That is exactly the “simplistic moralising” I claim to dislike; the reviewer has caught me doing the thing I warn against. The conflict is tragic because two legitimate claims collide, not because one party refuses to universalise a slogan.

But I won’t simply paste Reviewer A’s replacement, which is competent but a touch press-release. I’ll write it as a collision of rights and leave the moral tension live without the pointing finger:

“Never again — nie wieder — wurde zur Staatsräson Israels. Die Tragik des Konflikts liegt darin, dass die Sicherheit des einen Volkes auf Kosten der nationalen Selbstbestimmung des anderen errichtet wurde: zwei historische Rechte, die einander im Weg stehen. Das ist bis heute ungelöst.”

“Fundament”-Schlussformel (B’s priority item 3). Accept the de-teleologising. I keep the image once but immediately qualify it, as the Einleitung already does with “Katalysator und Legitimationsbeschleuniger.” The opening of the Schluss should not assert more than the chapter proved. Reword to: “Ohne den Holocaust sind die Dynamik und die internationalen Mehrheiten von 1947/48 kaum zu erklären. Er war Katalysator und Beschleuniger — nicht die alleinige Ursache.” This keeps consistency with the Einleitung and with point 8 below.

6. Logical / historical precisions#

“blind für die Gefahr” (Reviewer A — hindsight bias). Accept. This is a good catch and it matters methodologically. Calling the assimilated Jews “blind” is exactly the Hindsight Bias I would object to in someone else’s manuscript: their expectation that a constitutional state was irreversible was rational on the evidence available before 1933. Reviewer A’s replacement is good; I’ll use a close variant: “Diese tiefe Verwurzelung erschwerte es vielen, die Radikalität des Bruchs zu erfassen. Sie vertrauten auf den Rechtsstaat — eine Annahme, die sich als fatale Illusion erwies.”

Peel Commission — why it failed (Reviewer A). Accept. Without the outcome, 1937 reads as a smooth glide path. Add: the Arab leadership rejected partition outright; the Zionist leadership (Weizmann, Ben-Gurion) accepted it hesitantly as a basis for negotiation; the British shelved it (Woodhead Commission, 1938). One sentence, and the dynamic is honest.

“Die jüdische Neuzeit … endete 1897” (Reviewer B). Accept. I mean a caesura, not the end of an epoch. Reword the Einleitung to “der jüdischen Neuzeit, deren politische Wende der Erste Zionistische Kongress 1897 markierte.”

“Eichmann-Prozess brach das Schweigen” — prior steps (Reviewer B, Reviewer C). Accept a qualification. Yad Vashem (1953) and the Kastner trial (1954/57) preceded it, and Kastner already produced public testimony. The Eichmann trial broke the remaining silence and made the testimony society-wide. Fix: “durchbrach das verbleibende Schweigen und machte die Zeugnisse gesamtgesellschaftlich präsent.” I’ll add half a sentence noting Yad Vashem (1953) and the Kastner affair as earlier markers.

Soviet alliierte Kenntnis “reichlich Informationen” (Reviewer B). Accept the precision. For the 1939–42 span “reichlich” overstates; knowledge became substantial in 1942 (Riegner telegram, Polish reports) but was fragmentary and partly disbelieved before. Fix: “Ab 1942 liefen belastbare Hinweise und Berichte zusammen.” I keep the dry “spiegelte andere Prioritäten” line — Reviewer C explicitly praises it, and it states a fact, not a sermon.

7. Reviewer C’s additions — accept selectively#

I am wary of the maximalist reviewer who wants every adjacent fact included. Length is a cost, and this chapter is already long. I accept additions that close a logical gap and decline those that merely enrich.

Economic/bureaucratic barriers to flight (affidavits, Reichsfluchtsteuer). Accept, briefly. This closes a real gap: I say “other countries didn’t want them,” but the wall was also financial and bureaucratic. One sentence: “Selbst wer ein Zielland fand, scheiterte oft an Devisenbeschränkungen, der Reichsfluchtsteuer und an Bürgschaftsforderungen der Aufnahmeländer — die USA verlangten Affidavits, die für die meisten unerreichbar waren.” (I’ll drop C’s precise “$5,000,” which varied by case and would feign a precision I can’t source cleanly.)

Soviet denial of the specifically Jewish dimension (JAFC, “Soviet citizens”). Accept, briefly, as a footnote-weight half-sentence, because it genuinely explains why Soviet figures are contested and how the murders were recorded. Not a paragraph.

Explicit Holocaust → Nakba bridge. Partly accept. My existing paragraph (“Die Palästinenser haben den Holocaust nicht begangen — sie bezahlten dennoch einen Teil seines Preises”) already states the moral asymmetry, and it does so more economically than C’s proposed insertion. The Nakba itself belongs to the next chapter (1948). I’ll add only a one-clause forward pointer rather than import the 700,000 figure here, to avoid pre-empting the chapter that has to handle it properly.

McCloy / War Refugee Board / Breitman (“political not technical”). Decline as written; accept a trim. My passage already lays out the technical, strategic and moral arguments and ends “Dass er nicht stattfand, bleibt eine historische Last.” Reviewer C wants me to come down harder on “political, not technical.” But the honest historiographical position is that the refusal was both — the technical objections were real (the gas chambers were small, dispersed targets; rails are repaired in days), and the priority-setting was political. Tilting the verdict to “weniger Machbarkeit als Prioritätensetzung” overstates a contested point. I’ll add one clause noting that precision raids on comparably small targets did occur in 1944, which lets the reader weigh it — without my supplying the conclusion. That respects the reader the way the apricot essay recommends.

“sofortige oder schnelle Tötung” redundant (Reviewer C). Refute. The distinction is not redundant; it is real. At Treblinka/Belzec/Sobibor death was within hours (“sofort”). At Auschwitz, selection diverted a minority to labour and slower death (“schnell” but not immediate). Collapsing both to “sofortige Tötung” would be less accurate. I keep both, perhaps as “sofortige oder rasche Tötung.”

Passive → active (Reviewer C). Accept where it assigns agency. “Es wurde nicht bombardiert” → “Die Alliierten bombardierten die Gleise nicht” is a genuine improvement: responsibility has a subject. Same for “Es gab Widerstand” → “Jüdische Gruppen leisteten Widerstand.” Good, cheap fixes.

Drop the Arendt quote in favour of Stangneth (Reviewer C). Refute. The Arendt/Stangneth tension is the interesting thing, and my text already states that Stangneth relativised Arendt and that Eichmann was “wahrscheinlich beides — aber mehr Ideologe als Arendt annahm.” Deleting Arendt to assert Stangneth as settled fact would flatten a live debate into a verdict — the opposite of what I do elsewhere in the book. Keep both. (Note: this comment also partly moves with the Eichmann material, which stays in this chapter.)

8. Bias checks#

Protestant perspective: Barmen ignored the Jews; Stuttgart 1945 didn’t name antisemitism (Reviewer C). Accept — but in the relocated post-1965/aftermath chapter, since most of it lives there now. The one part that stays here (the silence of the churches 1933–45) should indeed note that the Barmer Erklärung defended church autonomy, not the Jews. My draft already says exactly this (“widerstand, aber vor allem zur Verteidigung ihrer eigenen Unabhängigkeit, nicht primär zur Verteidigung der Juden”), so the substance is present; I’ll add the Stuttgart 1945 point as the closing note of the in-chapter section.

Half-sentence on church-organised rescue (Italy/Hungary) for balance (Reviewer B). Accept. My draft already names individual Catholic rescuers (Lichtenberg, Galen on euthanasia) and the Vatican’s case-by-case help. I’ll add a clause on organised local help (Italian clergy, Budapest 1944) to keep the balance Reviewer B asks for.

Summary of what I’m doing#

Largest change: split Section 8. Keep the pre-1948 theological Vorgeschichte (Augustine → Luther → liturgy → silence of the churches), move the post-1965 reconciliation arc to a later thematic chapter. Drop the apologetic disclaimer entirely.

Accepted factual fixes: Karski/Churchill; Kennzeichnungs-chronology 1938/41; 1932 voting; Auschwitz bombing (“Kollateraltreffer”); emigration base (Altreich + Austria); Denmark numbers; Kielce widened-and-hedged; table note (1939 borders, Germany excl. Austria); Soviet-figure caveat.

Accepted style/logic fixes: “blind” → hindsight-aware rewording; Peel outcome added; “Neuzeit endete” → caesura; Eichmann “broke remaining silence” + Yad Vashem/Kastner; Évian/Wannsee/Exodus adjectives; “Never again” and “Fundament” de-moralised and de-teleologised; selected passives → active; define “Legitimationsbeschleuniger”; add flight-barriers and Soviet-denial half-sentences.

Refused, with reasons: “grausame Frage” → “unsensibel” (weaker and off-register; drop the adjective instead); “falsch” → “unzutreffend” (over-correction); cut Arendt for Stangneth (flattens a live debate); “sofortige oder schnelle” as redundant (real distinction); Reviewer C’s “political not technical” verdict on the bombing (overstates a contested point); amputating Section 8 at 1945 rather than splitting it.

One general remark to all three reviewers: the most valuable comments were the ones that caught me violating my own rules — the hindsight “blind,” the wagging-finger ending, the empty “bemerkenswert.” Those are worth more than the catalogue of adjacent facts I could have included, most of which would have made the chapter heavier without making it truer.
