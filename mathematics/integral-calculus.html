Path Integrals#

Let \(C\) be a smooth curve in \(\mathbb{R^n}\) and \(\boldsymbol{t}(x)\) the normal tangent vector (length \(= 1\)) at \(x \in C\). Let \(X = \{x_0=a, x_1, \ldots, x_n = b\}, x_k \in C\) be a polygon approximating \(C\). We define

\[\begin{split}\Delta_k &= x_k - x_{k-1} \\ \mu(X) &= \min \| \Delta_k \| \\ \boldsymbol{t}_k &= \frac{\Delta_k}{\| \Delta_k \|}\end{split}\]

The quantity \(\mu(X)\) is called the granularity of \(X\). As \(C\) is smooth, the approximations

\[\begin{split}|C| &\approx \sum_{k=1}^n \| \Delta_k \| \\ \boldsymbol{t}_k &\approx \boldsymbol{t}(x_k)\end{split}\]

become arbitrairily good as \(\mu(X)\) approaches zero. We consider a differentiable scalar field \(f\) and a vector field \(F\) on \(C\).

\[\begin{split}f &: C &\to \mathbb{R} \\ \partial f &: C &\to \mathbb{R^n} \\ F &: C &\to \mathbb{R^n}\end{split}\]

Definition 30 (Path Integrals)

(i) The integral of \(f\) along a smooth curve \(C\) is defined as

\[\int_C f \, ds \approx \sum_{k=1}^n f(x_k) \| \Delta_k \|\]

If \(f\) is the density of the material from which \(C\) is made then \(\int_C f \, ds\) is the weight of \(C\). With \(f = 1\) we get the arc length \(|C| = \int_C \, ds\).

(ii) Substituting \(F \cdot \boldsymbol{t}\) for \(f\), we get the definition of the integral of a vector field

\[\begin{split}\int_C F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds &\approx \sum_{k=1}^n F(x_k) \frac{\Delta_k}{\| \Delta_k \|} \| \Delta_k \| \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^n F(x_k) \Delta_k\end{split}\]

Theorem 41 (Path Independence)

(i) For any differentiable function \(f\) it holds that

\[\int_C \partial f \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds = f(b) - f(a)\]

This property is called path independence. It implies that integrals of gradients along closed curves are zero.

(ii) Let F be a vector field. If, for any closed curve \(C\), we have

\[\int_C F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds = 0\]

then \(F\) is a gradient: \(F = \partial f\) for some differentiable function \(f\).

Proof. (i) The definition of \(\partial f\) tells us

\[\partial f(x_k) \cdot \Delta_k \approx f(x_k) - f(x_{k-1}) \]

Summing over \(k\) gives:

\[\sum_{k=1}^n \partial f(x_k) \cdot \Delta_k = \sum_{k=1}^n f(x_k) - f(x_{k-1})\]

The left-hand side represents the definition of the path integral. The right-hand side is a telescoping sum. We get

\[\int_C \partial f \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds = f(b) - f(a)\]

(ii) For any \(x, y \in \mathbb{R^n}\), let \(C_{xy}\) denote a smooth curve from \(x\) to \(y\).
Take an arbitrary \(a \in \mathbb{R^n}\) and define

\[f(x) = \int_{C_{ax}} F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds\]

which is independent of \(C_{ax}\). Then, for small \(h\),

\[\begin{split}f(x + h) - f(x) &= \int_{C_{x,x+h}} F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds \\ \approx F(x) \cdot h\end{split}\]

which means that

\[\partial f = F\]

Now let \(C\) be defined by a parameter function \(x\).

\[\begin{split}x &: [0, T] \to \mathbb{R^n} \\ C &= x([0,T])\end{split}\]

We assume that \(\dot{x}(t) \neq 0\) on \([0, T]\).

Theorem 42 (Parameterized Curves)

(i) The tangent vector is given by

\[\boldsymbol{t}(t) = \frac{\dot{x}(t)}{\| \dot{x}(t) \|}\]

(ii) The scalar path integral is given by

\[\begin{split}\int_C f \, ds &= \int_0^T f(x(t)) \| \dot{x}(t) \| \, dt \\ \\ ds &= \| \dot{x} \| \, dt\end{split}\]

(iii) The vectorial path integral is given by

\[\begin{split}\int_C F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds &= \int_0^T F(x(t)) \cdot \dot{x}(t) \, dt \\ \\ \boldsymbol{t} \, ds &= \dot{x} \, dt\end{split}\]

(iv) The integral of a gradient along a parametrized curve \(C\), is given by

\[\int_C \partial f \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds = f(x(T)) - f(x(0))\]

Proof. (i) TODO

(ii) Let \([t_k]\) be a partition of \([0, T]\), and \({x_k} = x(t_k)\). Then

\[\| \dot{x}(t_k) \| \approx \frac{\| \Delta_k \|}{t_k - t_{k-1}}\]
\[\begin{split}\int_C f \, ds &\approx \sum_{k=1}^n f(x_k) \frac{\| \Delta_k \|}{t_k - t_{k-1}} \, (t_k - t_{k-1}) \\ &\approx \int_0^T f(x(t)) \| \dot{x}(t) \| \, dt \end{split}\]

(iii)

\[\int_C F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds = \int_0^T F \frac{\dot{x}}{\| \dot{x} \|} \, ds = \int_0^T F(x(t)) \cdot \dot{x}(t) \, dt\]

(iv)

\[\int_C \partial f \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds = \int_0^T \partial f(x(t)) \cdot \dot{x}(t) \, dt = f(x(T)) - f(x(0))\]

Surface Integrals#

Let \(A\) be a smooth surface in \(\mathbb{R^3}\) and \(\boldsymbol{n}(x)\) the outer normal vector (length \(= 1\)) at \(x \in A\). Let \(X = \{x_{00}, \ldots, x_{mn} \}, x_{jk} \in A\) be a polyhedron approximating \(A\). We define

\[\begin{split}A_{jk} &= [x_{jk}, x_{j-1, k}] \times [x_{jk}, x_{j, k-1}] \\ a_{jk} &= (x_{jk} - x_{j-1, k}) \times (x_{jk} - x_{j, k-1}) \\ \mu(X) &= \min_{jk} \| a_{jk} \| \\ \boldsymbol{n}_{jk} &= \frac{a_{jk}}{\| a_{jk} \|}\end{split}\]

The quantity \(\mu(X)\) is called the granularity of \(X\). As \(A\) is smooth, the approximations

\[\begin{split}|A| &\approx \sum_{j,k=1}^{m,n} \| a_{jk} \| \\ \boldsymbol{n}_{jk} &\approx \boldsymbol{n}(x_{jk})\end{split}\]

become arbitrarily good as \(\mu(X)\) approaches zero. We consider a differentiable scalar field \(f\) and a vector field \(F\) on \(A\).

\[\begin{split}f &: A &\to \mathbb{R} \\ \partial f &: A &\to \mathbb{R^3} \\ F &: A &\to \mathbb{R^3}\end{split}\]

Definition 31 (Surface Integral)

(i) The integral of \(f\) over a smooth surface \(A\) is defined as

\[\int_A f \, dS \approx \sum_{j,k=1}^{m,n} f(x_{jk}) \| a_{jk} \|\]

provided that the approximation becomes arbitrarily good as \(\mu(X)\) approaches zero.

If \(f\) is the density of the material from which \(A\) is made, then \(\int_A f \, dS\) is the weight of \(A\). With \(f = 1\) we get the surface \(|A| = \int_A \, dS\).

(ii) Substituting \(F(x) \cdot \boldsymbol{n}(x)\) (the flow density of \(F\) through \(A\) at \(x\)) for \(f\), we get the definition of the integral of a vector field \(F\), or the flow of \(F\) through \(A\).

\[\begin{split}\int_A F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS &= \lim_{\mu(X) \to 0} \sum_{j,k=1}^n F(x_{jk}) \frac{a_{jk}}{\| a_{jk} \|} \| a_{jk} \| \\ &= \lim_{\mu(X) \to 0} \sum_{j,k=1}^n F(x_{jk}) a_{jk}\end{split}\]

This reads as: The integral of the flow density over \(A\) equals the flow through \(A\).

We recall that the curl of a vector field is defined as

\[\begin{split}\partial \times F = \begin{bmatrix} \partial_2 F_3 - \partial_3 F_2 \\ \partial_3 F_1 - \partial_1 F_3 \\ \partial_1 F_2 - \partial_2 F_1 \end{bmatrix}\end{split}\]

Theorem 43 (Curl)

The curl of \(F\) is characterized by the defining property: Let \(x \in A\) and \(U \subset A\) be a neighbourhood of \(x\). The approximation

\[(\partial \times F)(x) \cdot \boldsymbol{n}(x) \approx \frac{1}{| U |}\int_{\partial U} F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds\]

becomes arbitrarily good as \(|U|\) approaches zero.

Proof. see Schey, p. 75

Theorem 44 (Stokes’ Theorem)

\[\int_A (\partial \times F) \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS = \int_{\partial A} F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds\]

Proof. Theorem 43 tells us, with \(U = A_{jk}\),

\[(\partial \times F)(x_{jk}) \cdot \frac{a_{jk}}{\| a_{jk} \|} \| a_{jk} \| \approx \int_{\partial A_{jk}} F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds\]

Note that \(\frac{a_{jk}}{\| a_{jk} \|} = \boldsymbol{n}_{jk} \approx \boldsymbol{n}(x_{jk}) \). Summing over \(k\) gives:

\[\sum_{j,k=1}^n(\partial \times F)(x_{jk}) \cdot \| a_{jk} \| \approx \sum_{j,k=1}^n \int_{\partial A_{jk}} F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds\]

The left-hand side represents the definition of the surface integral. The right-hand side is a telescoping sum because the sides of neighbouring rectangles facing each other cancel each other out, leaving only the sides facing outside. These sides approximate the boundary \(\partial A\). To be honest, it requires a certain amount of \(\epsilon\)-ceremony to rigourosly prove the last statement but you have got the idea. Thus

\[\int_A (\partial \times F) \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS = \int_{\partial A} F \cdot \boldsymbol{t} \, ds\]

Now let \(A\) be defined by a smooth parameter function \(x\). Let

\[\begin{split}R &= [0, U] \times [0, V] \subseteq \mathbb{R^2} \\ x &: R \to \mathbb{R^3} \\ A &= x(R) \end{split}\]

We assume that

\[\| \partial_u x \times \partial_v x \| \neq 0\]

on \(R\). Let \(\{u_j\}\) be a partition of \([0, U]\), \(\{v_k\}\) one of \([0, V]\), and \(R_{jk}\) the rectangle with upper right corner \((u_j, v_k)\). Let

\[x_{jk} = x(u_j, v_k) \]

Then \(X = [x_{00}, \ldots, x_{mn}]\) is a polyhedron covering \(A\).

Theorem 45 (Parameterized Surfaces)

(i) The normal vector at \(x(u, v)\) is given by

\[\boldsymbol{n}(u, v) = \frac{\partial_u x(u,v) \times \partial_v x(u,v)}{\| \partial_u x(u,v) \times \partial_v x(u,v) \|}\]

(ii) The scalar integral is given by

\[\begin{split}\int_A f \, dS &= \int_{R} f(x(u,v)) \, \| \partial_u x \times \partial_v x \| \, d(u,v) \\ \\ dS &= \| \partial_u x \times \partial_v x \| \, d(u,v)\end{split}\]

(iii) The flow integral is given by

\[\begin{split}\int_A F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS &= \int_{R} F(x(u,v)) \cdot (\partial_u x \times \partial_v x) \, d(u,v) \\ \\ \boldsymbol{n} \, dS &= (\partial_u x \times \partial_v x) \, d(u,v)\end{split}\]

Proof. (i) TODO

(ii) We recall that, for any two vectors \(a\) and \(b\), the surface of the parallelogram spanned by \(a\) and \(b\) is given by

\[\| a \times b \| = \| a \| \, \| b \| \sin \alpha \]

where \(\alpha\) is the angle between \(a\) and \(b\). Now let \(\alpha_{jk}\) be the angle between \(x_{jk} - x_{j-1, k}\) and \(x_{jk} - x_{j, k-1}\). The key observation is this:

\[\begin{split}\frac{\| a_{jk} \|}{(u_j - u_{j-1}) (v_k - v_{k-1})} &= \frac{\| (x_{jk} - x_{j-1, k}) \times (x_{jk} - x_{j, k-1}) \|}{(u_j - u_{j-1}) (v_k - v_{k-1})} \\ &= \frac{(x_{jk} - x_{j-1, k}) (x_{jk} - x_{j, k-1})}{(u_j - u_{j-1})(v_k - v_{k-1})} \sin(\alpha_{jk}) \\ &\approx \| \partial_u x(u_j, v_k) \| \, \| \partial_v x(u_j, v_k) \| \sin(\alpha_{jk}) \\ &\approx \| \partial_u(u_j, v_k) x \times \partial_v x(u_j, v_k) \| \end{split}\]

Thus

\[\begin{split}\int_A f \, dS &\approx \sum_{j,k=1}^{m,n} f(x_k) \| a_{jk} \| \\ &= \sum_{j,k=1}^{m,n} f(x_k) \frac{\| a_{jk} \|}{(u_j - u_{j-1}) \cdot(v_k - v_{k-1})} (u_j - u_{j-1}) (v_k - v_{k-1}) \\ &\approx \sum_{j,k=1}^{m,n} f(x_k) \| \partial_u x(u_j, v_k) \times \partial_v x(u_j, v_k) \| (u_j - u_{j-1}) (v_k - v_{k-1}) \\ &\approx \int_{R} f(x(u,v)) \, \| \partial_u x \times \partial_v x \| \, d(u,v) \end{split}\]

(iii)

\[\begin{split}\int_A F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS &= \int_{R} F(x(u,v)) \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, \| \partial_u x \times \partial_v x \| \, d(u,v) \\ &= \int_{R} F(x(u,v)) \cdot \frac{\partial_u x \times \partial_v x}{\|\partial_u x \times \partial_v x\|} \| \partial_u x \times \partial_v x \| \, d(u,v) \\ &= \int_{R} F(x(u,v)) \cdot (\partial_u x \times \partial_v) \, d(u,v) \end{split}\]

Volume Integrals#

Let \(\Omega\) be a volume in \(\mathbb{R^3}\) whose surface \(\partial \Omega\) is closed and smooth. . Let \(C = [c_{0}, \ldots, c_{n}]\) be a set of disjoint cubes covering \(\Omega\), that is

\[\Omega \subseteq \bigcup_{k} c_k\]

We define the granularity of \(C\) as

\[\mu(C) = \min_{k} \text{len}(c_k)\]

where \(\text{len}(c_k)\) is the edge length of \(c_k\). As \(\partial \Omega\) is smooth, the approximation

\[\begin{split}|\Omega| \approx \sum_{k=1}^{n} | c_k | \\\end{split}\]

becomes arbitrarily good as \(\mu(C)\) approaches zero.

We consider a differentiable scalar field \(f\) on \(\Omega\).

\[\begin{split}f : \Omega &\to \mathbb{R} \\\end{split}\]

Definition 32 (Volume Integrals)

The integral of \(f\) over \(\Omega\) is defined as

\[\int_{\Omega} f \, dV \approx \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(x_{k}) \, | c_{k} |\]

provided that the approximation becomes arbitrarily good as \(\mu(C)\) approaches zero.

If \(f\) is the density of the material from which \(\Omega\) is made, then \(\int_{\Omega} f \, dV\) is the weight of \(\Omega\). With \(f = 1\) we get the volume \(|\Omega| = \int_{\Omega} \, dV\).

We recall that the divergence of a vector field is defined as

\[\partial \cdot F = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \partial_i F_i\]

Theorem 46 (Divergence)

Let \(U\) be a small neighbourhood of \(x\) with a smooth surface. The approximation

\[(\partial \cdot F)(x) \approx \frac{1}{| U |}\int_{\partial U} F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS\]

becomes arbitrarily good as \(|U|\) approaches zero,

Proof. We consider a small cube \(Q\) with side lengths \(s_1, s_2, s_3\). Let \(\boldsymbol{e}_i\) be the i-th unitvector. We get for \(i= 1, 2, 3\)

\[\int_{\partial Q} F_i \cdot \boldsymbol{e}_i \, dS \approx \frac{F_i(x + s_i \boldsymbol{e}_i)}{s_i} s_1 s_2 s_3\]

Summing over \(i\) gives

\[\int_{\partial Q} F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS = (\partial F_1 + \partial F_2 + \partial F_3) \, |Q| = (\partial \cdot F) \, |Q|\]

Theorem 47 (Gauss’ Theorem)

\[\int_{\Omega} (\partial \cdot F) \, dV = \int_{\partial \Omega} F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS\]

Proof. Theorem 46 tells us, with \(U = c_k\),

\[(\partial \cdot F)(x_k) \, | c_k | \approx \int_{\partial c_k} F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS\]

Summing over \(k\) gives:

\[\sum_{k=1}^n (\partial \cdot F)(x_k) \, | c_k | \approx \sum_{k=1}^n \int_{\partial c_k} F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS\]

The left-hand side represents the definition of the volume integral. The right-hand side is a telescoping sum because the sides of neighbouring cubes facing each other cancel each other out, leaving only the sides facing outside. These sides approximate the boundary \(\partial \Omega\). Thus

\[\int_{\Omega} (\partial \cdot F)(x) \, dV \= \int_{\partial \Omega} F \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS \]

Now let \(\Psi \subset \mathbb{R^3}\) be another volume and

\[\begin{split}x &: \Psi \to \mathbb{R^3} \\ \Omega &= x(\Psi) \end{split}\]

where \(x\) is differentiable and

\[\det (\partial \otimes x) \neq 0\]

on \(Z\).

Theorem 49 (Green’s Formula)

Let \(f, g: \mathbb{R^3} \to \mathbb{R}\) be scalar fields, \(A \subset \mathbb{R^3}\) a smooth surface, and \(\Omega \subset \mathbb{R^3}\) a volume with a smooth surface.

(i) Product Rule

\[\partial \cdot (f \, \partial g) = f \partial^2 g + \partial f \partial g \]

(ii)

\[\int_A (f \, \partial g) \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS = \int_A f \, \frac{\partial g}{\partial \boldsymbol{n}} \, dS\]

(iii) Green’s Formula

\[\int_{\Omega} (f \, \partial^2 g - g \, \partial^2 f) \, dV = \int_{\partial \Omega} f \, \frac{\partial g}{\partial \boldsymbol{n}} - g \, \frac{\partial f}{\partial \boldsymbol{n}} \, dS\]

Proof. (i) Note that

\[\partial^2 f = \partial_1^2 f + \partial_2^2 f + \partial_3^2 f\]

and apply the plain product rule.

(ii)

\[\begin{split}\int_A (f \, \partial g) \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS &\approx \sum_{j,k}^{m,n} f(x_{jk})\partial g(x_{jk}) \cdot a_{jk} \\ &= \sum_{j,k}^{m,n} f(x_{jk}) \,(\partial g(x_{jk}) \cdot \boldsymbol{n}_{jk}) \| a_{jk} \| \\ &\approx \int_A f(x) \, (\partial g(x) \cdot \boldsymbol{n}(x)) \, dS \\ &= \int_A f \, \frac{\partial g}{\partial \boldsymbol{n}} \, dS\end{split}\]

(iii) We know from (i) that

\[f \, \partial^2 g - g \, \partial^2 f = \partial \cdot (f \, \partial g) - \partial \cdot (g \, \partial f)\]

Theorem 47 gives us, using (ii)

\[\int_{\Omega} \partial \cdot (f \, \partial g) = \int_{\partial \Omega} f \, \partial g \cdot \boldsymbol{n} \, dS = \int_{\partial \Omega} f \, \frac{\partial g}{\partial \boldsymbol{n}} \, dS\]

which yields the result

\[\int_{\Omega} (f \, \partial^2 g - g \, \partial^2 f) \, dV = \int_{\partial \Omega} (f \, \frac{\partial g}{\partial \boldsymbol{n}} - g \, \frac{\partial f}{\partial \boldsymbol{n}}) \, dS\]
