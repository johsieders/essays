# My questions in this chat

Claude, Time and Calendar

1. The tropical year is about 365.2425 days. What exactly is a day? How accurately is it defined?

2. What is the official time based on? When did it start? Is it measured in cesium-133-wiggles since X?

3. How do you coordinate 450 clocks around the globe? You have to know exactly the distance to each clock; you are verging on the simultaneity issue.

4. The Earth bulges at the equator; so, time at the equator is slower than at the poles (better: seen from the equator, time progresses faster at the poles). So, what is the rotating geoid?

5. I would like to see a good visualization of the revolving Earth orbiting the Sun, with rotation plane, equinoxes and so on.

6. Philosophical question: Measuring time depends on events that occur at (very) regular intervals. What if there are none? What do philosophers say? No measurement, no time?

7. Look at a cesium-clock (or any other clock): Can you rule out that after each click (or each 100th) the world stands still for an unmeasurable amount of time? My guess: (a) you cannot (b) absurd hypotheses are ruled out by Occam's razor.

8. Imagine an empty universe, void: no time because nothing happens, no distance because there are no objects around whose distance one could measure. There is a time as soon as there is a clock and a distance as soon as there is a ruler. Right?

9. What remains of GR in a void universe with no masses around, no gravity?

10. Back to the Earth. The Earth orbits the sun counterclockwise. Give me a concise argument why this way and not the other one.

11. When we measure time, we measure fractions (or multiples): the day is a fraction of the year, the hour a fraction of a day, a second is a multiple of a caesium-133 oscillation. Has there ever been the assumption of an absolute time outside of or above our earthly measurements? If so, who were the protagonists, and when did this assumption die?
