Below I work through the substantive points. I group them so as not to repeat myself across three reviewers who often raise the same issue.

## Factual corrections I accept

**1. Jewish share of the population around 1880 (all three reviewers).**
Accept. The 10% figure is too high for 1880. The Old Yishuv was roughly 20,000–25,000 out of ~450,000–500,000, i.e. 4–6%. The ~10% mark belongs to the eve of WWI. Fix: state "rund 5 Prozent Juden um 1880, gegen Ende der osmanischen Zeit etwa 10 Prozent", and mark the total as an estimate (osmanische Zählungen waren steuerlich motiviert, nicht demografisch). I will not pretend to McCarthy's precision in the body text but will name him in the sources.

**2. Balfour clause "weitgehend ignoriert" contradicts Section 4 (gemini, openai).**
Accept — this is a real internal inconsistency, and the sharper of the two points. Section 4 makes the clause the fulcrum of British policy swings; the intro sentence says it was ignored. I will delete "Davon abgesehen wurde er in der Praxis weitgehend ignoriert" and instead note that the clause covered *civil and religious*, not *political/national* rights (openai's correct refinement), and that it became precisely the lever for later restrictions. That resolves the contradiction and improves accuracy in one stroke.

**3. Sykes–Picot / Mosul (gemini, openai).**
Accept as a precision fix. Mosul was in the French zone in 1916 and was transferred to the British sphere in 1918–20 (Clemenceau–Lloyd George, formalised around San Remo, in exchange for a share in Turkish Petroleum). One clause added. I will not expand into the A/B-zone taxonomy — that is more granularity than the chapter's argument needs.

**4. "Palästina … von Anfang an unterlief" overpointed (openai).**
Partially accept. I will soften to "was London faktisch zugunsten eigener Kontrolle auslegte". The substance (Britain did not treat Palestine as a neutral international zone) stands.

**5. Fifth Aliya "überwiegend aus Deutschland und Österreich" (openai).**
Accept. German/Austrian Jews dominated 1933–36 but a large share of the 250,000 came from Poland and the rest of Eastern Europe. Fix: "ein erheblicher Teil aus Deutschland und Österreich (vor allem 1933–36), daneben weiterhin aus Polen und Osteuropa".

**6. Mandate chronology: in force 1923 after Lausanne (openai).**
Accept. The Council approved in July 1922; it entered into force in September 1923 after Lausanne. I will write "1922 genehmigt, 1923 in Kraft".

**7. Struma sequence (openai, mistral).**
Accept. The current sentence compresses the story misleadingly. Correct sequence: Britain refused entry certificates; Turkey, unwilling to keep the ship, towed it back into the Black Sea; a Soviet submarine then torpedoed it. One survivor. Fix accordingly.

**8. Armistice "März 1949" too neat (openai).**
Accept. The agreements were staggered: Egypt 24 Feb, Lebanon 23 March, Jordan 3 April, Syria 20 July 1949. I will write "die Waffenstillstandsabkommen von 1949 (Februar bis Juli)".

**9. UdSSR recognition date (openai).**
Accept the precision: de jure 17 May. I'll keep "drei Tage später" only if I state the dates; simpler to say "wenige Tage später".

**10. "Elf Stunden später" (gemini accepts the framing; openai/mistral object).**
Accept the objection. The "11 minutes" for Truman is documented and worth keeping for its rhetorical bite. "Elf Stunden" is false precision and, worse, invites a symmetry that the facts do not support. Replace with "am folgenden Tag". This also weakens the "Drehbuch" impression, which is the right direction (see below).

## Factual points I refute or qualify

**11. JNF land "nie wieder an Nichtjuden" / "bis heute" too sweeping (openai).**
Partially accept, but the reviewer overstates the correction. The JNF charter restriction is real and was the operative reality for the period the chapter covers. The *Ka'adan* ruling (2000) and subsequent ILA arrangements complicate the present-day picture. Since this is a historical chapter, I will keep the charter clause as described for the Mandate era and add a one-clause footnote that the legal position has been contested since 2000. I will drop the unqualified "bis heute".

**12. al-Husseini "von den Briten eingesetzt" (mistral).**
Partially accept. mistral is right that he was elected by the Supreme Muslim Council and the Mufti selection in 1921 went through a process — but Herbert Samuel engineered that outcome (Husseini placed third in the electoral college; Samuel's administration arranged his appointment). So "von den Briten eingesetzt" is defensible shorthand but I will make it precise: "von der Mandatsverwaltung gegen das Wahlergebnis durchgesetzt". This is actually *more* damning of the British and *more* accurate.

**13. al-Husseini motive: "um sie in die Vernichtungslager zu schicken" is unwarranted motive attribution (openai, mistral).**
Accept the caution. Documented: he intervened to block emigration of Jewish children to Palestine; he knew the alternative. The inference about his intent to send them to the camps is probable but is an attribution I should not state as fact. Rewrite: "um ihre Auswanderung nach Palästina zu verhindern — im vollen Bewusstsein, welches Schicksal ihnen sonst drohte." That keeps the moral weight without claiming to read his mind.

**14. mistral: al-Husseini's significance "übertrieben"; he had no operational influence; other Arab nationalists (al-Gaylani) also cooperated with the Axis.**
Refute the framing, partly accept the addition. I never claim he influenced the Holocaust's machinery; I claim collaboration, propaganda, recruitment, and obstruction of child emigration — all documented. The chapter already separates the man from the people explicitly. The al-Gaylani comparison (anti-British alliance of convenience) is fair context and I can add one sentence — but it explains motive, it does not relativise the SS recruitment or the child-transport interventions, which were not anti-British acts. I will add the context and decline the implied softening.

**15. King David Hotel warning calls (gemini).**
Accept as a fact to include, refute the implied exculpation. The Irgun did telephone warnings; the British did not evacuate; responsibility for that failure is contested to this day. Including it improves precision. But gemini frames this as restoring symmetry with Deir Yassin — and that I reject. A warned bombing of a military/administrative headquarters and the shooting of villagers are different categories; noting the warning does not move King David toward Deir Yassin, it simply states what happened. I'll add the warning in one clause and leave the moral distinction intact.

**16. Irgun "rechtsextrem" → "revisionistisch-nationalistisch" (openai).**
Accept. "Revisionistisch-nationalistische Untergrundorganisation" is more precise and less of an anachronistic label. Change made.

**17. Deir Yassin "Kriegsverbrechen" — legal anachronism (openai); rape reports propagandistic (mistral).**
Partially accept. "Massaker" is the safer and sufficient term; I'll use it and drop the legal classification, since the applicable legal framework in April 1948 is genuinely arguable. I make no claim about rapes in the chapter, so mistral's point needs no change here — but I'll keep the casualty figure as "etwa 100–110", reflecting the convergence of Khalidi's and Morris's later revisions away from the original 254.

**18. Lod/Ramle: Ben-Gurion → Rabin command line "umstritten" (openai, mistral).**
Partially accept. The Rabin passage exists and was censored; the exact chain of command and Ben-Gurion's hand gesture are debated. I'll add "die Deutung der Befehlskette ist umstritten" rather than present it as settled.

## Logical and framing points

**19. "Drehbuch" is a strawman (gemini).**
Accept in substance. No serious historian claims a literal script. The section's *content* already argues exactly the reviewer's point — organised Yishuv vs. fragmented Arab coalition. The heading "War die Staatsgründung ein 'Drehbuch'?" sets up a question the reader probably wasn't asking. I'll retitle it "Vorbereitung gegen Improvisation" or similar, keeping the body. Combined with fixing "elf Stunden" (point 10), the unfounded conspiracy aroma disappears.

**20. The core thesis — "für ein europäisches Verbrechen mit ihrer Heimat bezahlte" — ignores the autonomous pre-1939 local conflict (gemini); "mit ihrer Heimat" too absolute, ~150,000 stayed (openai).**

This is the most important critique and I accept both halves.

- *Pre-existing conflict:* gemini is right that Jewish–Arab violence was already a zero-sum dynamic by the 1920s (Nebi Musa 1920, Jaffa 1921, Hebron 1929, the 1936–39 revolt). My framing risks suggesting the conflict was purely an imported European problem. The accurate claim is that the Holocaust *accelerated and made internationally irresistible* a conflict that had its own decades-old local dynamics. I'll adjust the intro and conclusion to say this, and — see point 22 — add the missing milestones that make it true in the body.
- *"mit ihrer Heimat":* openai is right. ~150,000 Arabs remained inside Israel in 1949. "mit einem großen Teil ihrer Heimat" is the honest formulation.

I will not adopt gemini's proposed replacement sentence verbatim — "existenzielle Notwendigkeit eines jüdischen Zufluchtsorts" smuggles in a conclusion. I'll keep the descriptive register.

**21. UN 181 "Land wegnehmen, das ihr gehörte" conflates demographic majority with private ownership (openai, mistral).**
Accept. Much of the partitioned area was state/communal land (former miri/mahlul), and Jews owned ~6–7% privately. The legitimate grievance is about national self-determination and demographic majority, not wholesale private expropriation. I'll rephrase: "Man entzog einer Bevölkerungsmehrheit gegen ihren Willen die Selbstbestimmung über einen Großteil des Landes, auf dem sie lebte" — which is the defensible claim and avoids the property confusion. I'll also add the within-the-Jewish-state figures gemini supplied (~500,000 Jews, ~400,000 Arabs plus Bedouin), since they make the demographic knife-edge concrete and explain why "transfer" was viewed as a "necessity."

**22. Missing milestones: 1920/1921/1929/1936–39 revolt, Peel 1937 with the transfer debate (openai, mistral).**
Accept — this is the most consequential *omission*. These events explain the British policy swings (point 2 of the inconsistency), the radicalisation on both sides, and the autonomy of the local conflict (point 20). I'll add a compact subsection in Section 3 or 4 covering Nebi Musa, Jaffa, Hebron 1929, and the 1936–39 revolt with the Peel Commission's first partition proposal and its explicit transfer recommendation. This single addition does more for the chapter than any wording change.

**23. Arab institutions existed (Arab Higher Committee 1936, All-Palestine Government 1948) (mistral).**
Accept as a corrective to "no institutions at all." My claim is *relative* organisational weakness, not total absence, but I overstate it. I'll add a sentence acknowledging the Arab Higher Committee (1936, banned after the revolt) and the powerless All-Palestine Government (1948) — which actually *strengthens* my thesis: attempts existed but were late, suppressed, or hollow.

**24. British "divide and rule" as an active factor, not just an "Eiertanz" (mistral).**
Partially accept. Britain did exploit the Husseini–Nashashibi rivalry, and the suppression of the 1936–39 revolt was brutal (mistral's "200 villages" figure I'd want to check before printing — I'll say "mit großer Härte" rather than cite an unverified number). But mistral overreaches in claiming the whole Mandate was *deliberate* division rather than contradiction. Both are true at different moments: structurally incoherent mandate *and* tactical divide-and-rule. I'll add a sentence on the latter without abandoning the former.

**25. Jewish flight from Arab lands 1948–56; Arab League boycott as counterpart to Avoda Ivrit (openai, mistral).**
Accept for the conclusion's balance. ~700,000–850,000 Jews left or were driven from Arab countries in the following years — a reciprocal displacement that belongs in the closing "Righteous Victims" balance. The Arab boycott as a counterpart to the Histadrut boycott is also fair. One or two sentences each; I won't let it grow into whataboutism.

**26. Mention binational-state alternative (Magnes, Brit Shalom) (mistral).**
Accept briefly. Worth one sentence to note that a binational option existed and had advocates on both margins, and why it never commanded a majority on either side. I decline mistral's invitation to speculate counterfactually ("hätte es funktioniert?") — that's not something the evidence answers.

**27. Plan Dalet: defensive plan vs. expulsion framework — present the contested reading (openai, mistral).**
Accept. I'll state both readings explicitly: a military securing-of-territory plan whose execution, in numerous documented cases, included expulsion; historians (Morris vs. Khalidi/Pappé) dispute whether expulsion was the design or a consequence. On mistral's request for the source of the Plan D quotation — fair; if I quote it I'll cite the text via Morris's reproduction rather than imply direct archival work.

**28. Power asymmetry in 1948: "Israel had no army, only militias" vs. regular Arab armies (mistral).**
Refute. This is the weakest point in the reviews. By May 1948 the Haganah/IDF fielded more troops than the combined Arab expeditionary forces actually committed, and was better organised and, after the spring/summer arms shipments (Czech weapons), better equipped. The "tiny militia vs. regular armies" picture is the traditional Israeli narrative that the New Historians dismantled — and the chapter relies on those very historians elsewhere. I will not adopt this framing; if anything the chapter should make the eventual balance clearer.

## Style points

**29. "Eiertanz", "Kolonialismus mit besserer PR", "strukturell unmöglich", "kein historisches Vorbild" (all three).**
Mostly refute, with one concession. These are the chapter's voice, and dry irony is intentional. "Kolonialismus mit besserer PR" is a compression I'll defend — it is accurate about the Mandate system's relation to the prewar colonial carve-up. "Eiertanz" I'll keep once and not repeat. The genuine fix is the heading "Drehbuch" (point 19) and "elf Stunden" (point 10), which create a *factual* misimpression — that's a different matter from register. I'd rather risk one reviewer finding me "salopp" than write "völkerrechtliche Bemäntelung kolonialer Interessen", which is exactly the kind of phrase that puts a reader to sleep.

**30. "Katastrophe" needs distancing (mistral).**
Refute. The text already does this: "bezeichnen Palästinenser als *Nakba* — Katastrophe." That *is* the attribution. No change.

**31. "Fehler" for al-Husseini's collaboration is an understatement; "moralisch nicht zu relativieren" is preaching (gemini).**
Partially accept. I'll drop "moralisch nicht zu relativieren" (the facts carry the weight; the editorial is redundant) and keep "Fehler" only in combination — "sein schwerster Fehler und moralischer Offenbarungseid" — so it reads as both strategic catastrophe and moral one, per gemini's better formulation, minus the sermon.

**32. Source transparency for figures (mistral).**
Accept lightly. This is a chapter for general readers, not an apparatus-heavy monograph, so I won't footnote every number. But for the genuinely contested ones — Deir Yassin toll, Plan D quotation, refugee phasing — I'll name whose estimate I'm using inline (Morris, Khalidi). That's proportionate.

---

**Net effect.** The two changes that matter most are substantive, not stylistic: (a) adding the 1920–1939 violence and the Peel transfer debate, which simultaneously fixes the Balfour inconsistency, the "imported European problem" framing, and the "Arabs had no institutions" overstatement; and (b) correcting the 1880 demographics and the partition-vs-private-property confusion. The "Eiertanz"/"Drehbuch" complaints are largely about register, and I'll concede only where the wording produces a false factual impression rather than merely an irreverent tone.
