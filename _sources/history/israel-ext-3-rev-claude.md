## Factual Errors & Corrections

**1. Bayezid II Quote — Accept (partial).** The chapter already flags the quote as historically uncertain. I accept the suggestion to sharpen the wording: I'd change "historisch nicht zweifelsfrei belegt" to something like "ein legendäres Diktum, das erst Generationen später in jüdischen Quellen auftaucht." **2. Sabbatai Zwi's followers not monolithic — Refute.** The chapter already does this. **3. Chmielnicki — why older estimates persisted — Accept.** Good suggestion. **4. Spinoza's cherem had non-philosophical causes — Refute (as stated).** The reviewer overstates a real scholarly debate. The actual text of the cherem cites "abominable heresies" and "monstrous deeds," and the precise reasons remain disputed; commercial/communal-political motives (the Amsterdam community's fragile standing with Dutch authorities) are a scholarly *hypothesis*, not established fact. Adding it as a confident corrective would introduce a contested claim, not fix an error. I'd at most add a hedged half-sentence, but I won't accept it as a correction of an error, because the chapter's account is not wrong.

**5. The point is fully present.

**2. German historical writing routinely uses historical present for vividness (e.g., Spinoza section). It would only be a defect if uncontrolled within a passage; I don't see that. No change.

**3. **4. Repetition of the integration paradox — Accept (partial).** It appears in the intro, then is explicitly recalled in section 9 ("Wie schon in der Einleitung konstatiert"). Minor tightening, not removal of the structural callback.
